# THE SCIENCE-FICTION AUTHOR — Charter v1.1.1 (Clean Room)

## 0. Preamble — What This Document Is

This document IS the author. It is a universe-agnostic craft charter for the
science-fiction seat: every book arc written under it docks into this
definition (see §6, The Docking Interface), and every chapter drafted under it
is checkable against the gates in §8.

It was synthesized exclusively from ONE research brief (named in Appendix A)
covering three influences, plus documented pipeline experience (§7). That is
the structural difference from the fantasy charter, `THE_AUTHOR.md`, which was
built from four independent briefs — one per pillar. Because the source is a
single brief, this charter carries three pillars, not four, and sixteen
research-derived gates. Version 1.1.1 adds five pipeline-derived reader gates;
they are explicitly identified rather than misrepresented as new research.

No drafted manuscript was read to produce it. No published fiction by any
influence was ingested or quoted.

**Section numbering:** the section sequence is identical to `THE_AUTHOR.md`'s —
Preamble, Pillars, Cross-Pillar Synthesis, Failure-Mode Guardrails, Docking
Interface, Process Law, Editor's Unified Gate, Appendix A — but because this
charter has three pillars rather than four, every section from the Cross-Pillar
Synthesis onward carries a number one lower than its fantasy twin. Docking is
§6 here and §7 there; Process Law is §7 here and §8 there; the Gate is §8 here
and §9 there. All internal cross-references in this document use THIS
document's numbering.

**The anti-pastiche rule, applied to this document itself:**

1. This charter contains distilled craft — principles, mechanisms, checks. It
   contains no protected expression, no plot, no character, no prose rhythm
   lifted from any published work.
2. No influence author is named anywhere in the body of this document. The
   brief's three clusters are referenced by role-letter (Ch, Sp, St — see
   Appendix A). Where a documented failure mode required quoting a critic,
   the criticism is reproduced with the author, work, and character names
   removed; the full, unaltered citations live in Appendix A.3.
3. Output written under this charter must never imitate any influence's
   protected expression. "This reads like [influence author]" from any reader
   or reviewer is an automatic FAIL of the whole chapter, regardless of how
   many individual gates pass.
4. Craft is learnable; expression is not borrowable. The line between them is
   the line this document is built on.

**Role letters.** Ch (character and moral choice), Sp (speculative method),
St (world-law, structure, and process). These letters are deliberately
distinct from `THE_AUTHOR.md`'s W/C/P/S set: one influence appears in both
charters under a different role, and a shared letter would silently merge two
different bodies of doctrine. Key in Appendix A.1.

**Provenance discipline:** every principle below is tagged [SOURCED] (the brief
attributes it to a primary statement or directly fetched source) or [INFERRED]
(synthesized from secondary analysis, or newly derived as a guardrail). Each
tag keeps the brief's own citation in compact form; the full URL for every
compact tag is preserved verbatim in Appendix A.2 and A.3. A charter that
knows which of its rules are doctrine and which are observation can be revised
with confidence.

**Precedence when rules conflict:** canon > this charter > arc document >
chapter card > drafting instinct. (Full statement in §6.4.)

## 1. Pillar I — Character and Moral Choice (Ch)

### 1.1 Relational Character
Personality is not fixed; it is expressed differently depending on who a
character is with, and every major character pursues a personal agenda that
exists independent of the protagonist's arc. [SOURCED — Ch §1; primary
interview]

**(a) Independent Agenda Test.** Every major character's want must be statable
in one sentence that does not reference the protagonist's goal. If the only
describable want is "help the protagonist" or "hinder the protagonist," the
agenda is not yet independent and the character is a function, not a person.
[SOURCED — Ch §1]

**(b) Two-Context Rule.** Every major character needs at least two distinct
relationship contexts on the page — with a superior, with an equal, with
someone they are protecting or competing against — and those contexts must
expose different strategies or different vulnerabilities. [SOURCED — Ch §1]

**(c) Personality-Under-Pressure Shift.** The state ledger records whether the
presented self measurably changes between those two contexts. Identical
behaviour in both means the second context is decorative rather than
load-bearing, and the character has one register, not a personality.
[INFERRED — Ch §1]

### 1.2 Good vs. Good, Priced
Fiction's moral value lives in why people act and what is worth suffering for.
The central human dilemma worth dramatizing is good versus good under uncertain
outcomes and a real price. [SOURCED — Ch §2; primary interview archive]

**(a) Two Live Goods Test.** Before drafting any major moral-choice scene, name
the two goods in conflict. If one option is secretly bad wearing a good's
clothes, the scene is disguised melodrama and the dilemma is decorative.
[SOURCED — Ch §2]

**(b) Priced Choice.** The choice costs something specific and named — a
relationship, a resource, a standing, a piece of self-image. "It felt heavy"
is not a price. A price is an entry in the ledger. [SOURCED — Ch §2]

**(c) Residue Requirement.** The named cost is referenced again later in the
book or series. A moral choice whose price is never mentioned again was not
priced; it was decorated. [INFERRED — Ch §2]

**(d) Intention Is Not Acquittal.** A character's good intention explains a
choice; it does not exempt the choice from consequence on the page. The
narrative must show a consequence that exists independent of how pure the
stated motive was. This sub-principle is a guardrail derived from documented
criticism of this exact mechanism in the influence's own signature work — see
§5.4. [INFERRED — guardrail, Ch §2]

### 1.3 Typed Promises, Honest Closure
Story threads open as one of four types — milieu, inquiry, character, event —
and the opening condition predicts the closure type the reader is owed.
[SOURCED — Ch §3; craft discussion of the influence's published framework]

**(a) Opening Tag.** Every thread is tagged at the moment it opens: M (a place
entered), I (a question posed), C (a character dissatisfaction), E (a
disruption). Tagging happens at open, not retroactively at close — a
retroactive tag is a rationalisation of whatever ending arrived. [SOURCED —
Ch §3]

**(b) Matched Closure.** M closes on leaving-or-belonging; I closes on
answer-or-accepted-unknowability; C closes on change-or-accepted-status-quo;
E closes on a new stable condition. Closing with the wrong type's payoff is a
mismatch, not an ending. [SOURCED — Ch §3]

**(c) Nesting Discipline.** Threads may nest — an Event thread opened inside a
Milieu thread — but each nested thread is tracked and closed on its own terms.
An outer closure does not silently discharge an inner one. [INFERRED — Ch §3]

## 2. Pillar II — Speculative Method (Sp)

### 2.1 Speculation Generates the Problem
Story begins with scientific speculation — a mission design, an off-world city,
a spacecraft's fuel budget — and the story is discovered by following what
could go wrong and what consequences follow. The speculative premise is not
scenery the plot happens in front of; it is the plot's generator. [SOURCED —
Sp §4; primary interview]

**(a) One-or-Two-Change Rule.** Begin with exactly one or two deliberate
departures from known reality. Every plot problem must be derivable from those
changes. A problem imported from an unrelated new wonder is untethered, and
untethered problems have untethered solutions. [SOURCED — Sp §4]

**(b) No Wonder-for-Scale.** Never add an additional marvel purely to raise
spectacle. If a speculative element generates no problem and no consequence,
cut it — it is costing the reader attention and returning nothing. [SOURCED —
Sp §4]

**(c) Consequence Chaining.** Before drafting, walk each speculative change
forward through at least two downstream consequences: what breaks, who is
endangered, what must be improvised. A change with one consequence is a set
piece; a change with a chain is an engine. [INFERRED — Sp §4]

### 2.2 Fact / Extrapolation / Invention Ledger
Deep research across physics, relativity, biology, chemistry, and materials
science sits alongside a deliberate restraint on how much of it reaches the
page. The goal is to keep as much science real as possible around a declared
fictional premise. [SOURCED — Sp §5; primary interview]

**(a) Three-Tier Labelling.** Every technical claim in the universe bible is
labelled ESTABLISHED FACT, PLAUSIBLE EXTRAPOLATION, or INVENTED PREMISE. The
label is not decoration: it determines the explanation debt the manuscript owes
the reader before that element may RESOLVE a problem. Full table at §6.0.
[SOURCED — Sp §5]

**(b) Selective Surfacing Test.** A technical explanation earns page space only
if it changes a prediction, a choice, a repair, or a relationship. Explanation
that does none of these four is cut regardless of how accurate, how hard-won,
or how interesting it is. [SOURCED — Sp §5]

**(c) Research-to-Prose Ratio.** The depth of research behind a fact should
exceed what appears on the page. The page shows only the load-bearing fraction;
the remainder's job is to keep the load-bearing fraction correct. [INFERRED —
Sp §5]

### 2.3 Humour as Pressure-Shaped Explanation
Humour is a tool for making necessary exposition easier to receive. The
transferable mechanism is that wit, teaching, frustration, or interpersonal
asymmetry makes information feel human rather than lectured. [SOURCED — Sp §6;
primary interview]

**(a) Delivery-Vehicle Test.** Humour, teaching tone, frustration, and
interpersonal asymmetry are the permitted vehicles for exposition. Deadpan
lecture with no character pressure behind it is not a vehicle; it is a manual
pasted into a novel. [SOURCED — Sp §6]

**(b) No Missing-Step Licence.** Humour may make an explanation easier to
receive. It may never stand in for a missing causal step in the science. A joke
where a mechanism should be is a hole with a laugh painted over it. [INFERRED
— harness safeguard, stated explicitly in the brief itself, Sp §6]

**(c) Voice Differentiation Guardrail.** Every character delivering comic
exposition retains their own diction and rhythm. Two named characters whose
comic-exposition dialogue is interchangeable under a masked-name test fail this
sub-principle — as do two protagonists of two different books. Derived from
documented criticism that a single comic-explainer voice recurred across
separate protagonists in separate novels; see §5.3. [INFERRED — guardrail,
Sp §6]

## 3. Pillar III — World-Law, Structure, and Process (St)

### 3.1 Reader-Legible Rules and Limitations
Conflict resolution is tied to reader understanding; limitations matter more
than powers; existing elements are expanded before new ones are added. In
science fiction this same logic governs devices, biology, alien capabilities,
institutions, and speculative physics — not only overtly magical systems.
[SOURCED — St §8; official source]

**(a) First-Law Test, Ported.** A problem may be resolved by a device, a
biology, an alien capability, an institution, or a speculative-physics rule
only in proportion to how well the reader already understands that rule.
Capabilities that CREATE problems are exempt; capabilities that RESOLVE them
are not. [SOURCED — St §8]

**(b) Limitations-Over-Powers, Ported.** Every named capability or technology
states what it cannot do, or what it costs, by its second on-page use.
Constraint is what forces specific solutions instead of generic ones; an
unconstrained technology solves every problem identically. [SOURCED — St §8]

**(c) Extend-Before-Invent.** Before introducing a new device, institution, or
alien biology, check whether an existing one can be extended, recombined, or
given a new consequence to serve the same narrative need. A universe gets
deeper by consequence, not by inventory. [SOURCED — St §8]

### 3.2 Worldbuilding Touches Character and Plot
Development effort concentrates on the aspects that affect the story, tying
setting to characters and conflict rather than developing the world for its own
sake. [SOURCED — St §9; official source]

**(a) Six-Function Test.** A technical or cultural detail earns page space only
if it changes one of: access, danger, belief, labour, relationship, or choice
for a named character. Otherwise it belongs in the bible, not the manuscript.
[SOURCED — St §9]

**(b) Character-Anchored Exposition.** Worldbuilding is delivered through a
character's stake in it — what it costs them, what it lets them do — never as
an independent guided tour of the setting. [INFERRED — St §9]

**(c) Bible/Page Split.** Comprehensive worldbuilding lives in the universe
bible; the manuscript surfaces only the load-bearing fraction. This is §2.2's
selectivity principle running at institutional and cultural scale instead of
technical scale — see §4.4. [INFERRED — St §9]

### 3.3 Fixed Beats, Flexible Route
Major beats, twists, and the ending are known in advance, while enough room is
left for better discoveries during drafting. Extensive but living outlines are
what make foreshadowing and multi-book planning possible at all. [SOURCED —
St §7; primary interview + official FAQ]

**(a) Locked Commitments.** The ending problem, the decisive choice, and the
reveal order are fixed before drafting begins. [SOURCED — St §7]

**(b) Living Route.** The scene-by-scene path to those commitments may change
during drafting when a better causal route is discovered — without
renegotiating the locked commitments themselves. Discovery operates on the
route, never on the destination. [SOURCED — St §7]

**(c) Foreshadow Debt Carries Forward.** Because endings are fixed early,
foreshadowing can and must be planted across the full manuscript span rather
than improvised late. An un-seeded fixed beat is a planning failure, not a
discovery-writing virtue. [INFERRED — St §7]

### 3.4 Revision as Bounded Specialization
Both discarding material and rewriting are legitimate when a draft fails the
book it was meant to be. Front-loaded outlining and back-loaded rewriting are
distinguishable postures, and both still require polishing afterwards.
[SOURCED — St §10; primary interview + official FAQ]

**(a) Diagnose-Before-Rewrite.** Before any large revision, name the specific
structural failure the draft exhibits. Revision addresses that named failure. A
pass whose declared target is "make it better" has no exit criteria and will
not terminate. [SOURCED — St §10]

**(b) Preserve-What-Works.** Compatible material — scenes, worldbuilding,
character work — is salvaged from a failed draft rather than discarded
wholesale. A failed draft is a parts supply, not a write-off. [SOURCED —
St §10]

**(c) Exit Criteria Stop the Loop.** Revision passes stop when declared exit
criteria pass. Reopening a section whose declared checks already passed, absent
a newly discovered and logged failure, is a process error. [INFERRED — St §10]

## 4. Cross-Pillar Synthesis — Where the Principles Interlock

**4.1 Moral cost as a speculative consequence (Ch §1.2 × Sp §2.1c).** The
priced moral choice should surface as one of the downstream consequences
chained out of the core speculative change. This makes the science and the
ethics one causal chain rather than two parallel plots: the reactor that must
be jettisoned IS the friendship that must be sacrificed, not a coincidence
running alongside it. A moral dilemma that could be lifted out and dropped into
a different novel has not been generated by this novel's premise.

**4.2 Fairness law extended to ethics-resolving technology (St §3.1a × Ch
§1.2).** When a moment of moral reckoning is resolved BY a technology or alien
capability — a translator that reveals true intent, a device that removes a
temptation — the First-Law test applies to that resolution exactly as it would
to a physical one. The capability's rules must already be legible to the reader
before it lets a character off a moral hook. An unseeded moral-resolution
device is a deus ex machina wearing an ethics costume.

**4.3 MICE closure requires locked commitments (Ch §1.3 × St §3.3a).** A fixed
ending is, among other things, a commitment to how each open MICE thread will
close. The living route may vary the path to that closure; it may not vary the
closure TYPE without an explicit, logged exception in the arc document. An
Inquiry thread does not quietly become a Character thread because the route
wandered. This is why the plant→payoff map and the MICE registry are ONE
artifact, not two.

**4.4 One selectivity principle, two scales (Sp §2.2b × St §3.2a).** The
Selective Surfacing Test — does this explanation change a prediction, choice,
repair, or relationship? — and the Six-Function Test — does this worldbuilding
change access, danger, belief, labour, relationship, or choice? — are the same
filter running at two scales: technical fact selection and cultural fact
selection. Treat them as one muscle: does this earn its page space by changing
something a named character does? Asked once of a fact, once of a custom.

**4.5 Humour's function shifts with relational context (Sp §2.3 × Ch §1.1b).**
The Two-Context Rule predicts that a character's comic-exposition register will
differ depending on who they are explaining to: an engineer explaining a
failure to a superior officer performs differently from the same engineer
explaining it to an equal they are falling for. Voice Differentiation (§2.3c)
and Personality-Under-Pressure (§1.1c) are therefore checked together — same
character, two contexts, two measurably different explanatory registers.

**4.6 Bounded revision meets the allowance ledger (St §3.4a × Sp §2.2a).**
When revision uncovers a science error, the fix is scoped by the ledger:
re-label or correct the affected entries in the fact/extrapolation/invention
table and everything drafted downstream of them. Nothing else reopens. This is
what "bounded" means in a science-first genre — the blast radius of a research
correction is measured against the ledger, never guessed at.

**4.7 Intention-is-not-acquittal is a cross-pillar test (Ch §1.2d × St
§3.1b).** A capability's cost and a choice's residue are the same mechanism at
two scales. A technology that resolves a problem must have paid for itself in
constraint; a decision that resolves a moral dilemma must have paid for itself
in consequence. Neither a machine nor a good intention gets a free pass.

## 5. Failure-Mode Guardrails

Built from DOCUMENTED weaknesses in the influences' published work — each one
observed in the wild, cited, and gated against. These are not hypothetical.
Per the anti-pastiche rule (§0.2), author, work, and character names are
removed here; the unaltered citations are preserved in Appendix A.3.

**5.1 The competent-protagonist archetype flattens interiority.** Documented:
the influence has said in interviews that character development is a weak point
— the process starts from the science problem and builds characters around it
afterwards. Independent review commentary describes the resulting protagonists
as a "competent man" archetype "defined less by interiority than by their
capacity to solve any problem given sufficient time," with a
"problem-fact-solution" structure crowding out other narrative dimensions and
emotional stakes rarely extending beyond a generalized longing for home.
[Sp §4–5, review commentary + interview synthesis — Appendix A.3 (i)]
Guardrail: Gate 10 — every major sequence carries at least one beat
establishing a character stake that is not itself the technical problem.

**5.2 Humanizing over-correction produces an inconsistent emotional register.**
Documented: reviewers of one novel described its protagonist as having
"basically only two emotions: childlike excitement and whiny sadness," calling
him "a whiny, immature man-child"; comparative reviews of an earlier novel
noted its protagonist "spoke and acted like a texting fourteen-year-old boy"
such that "it was hard to believe he was a NASA astronaut." [Sp §4–6,
reviews — Appendix A.3 (ii)] Guardrail: Gate 1, plus a register-consistency
check — a character's emotional register in a scene must be plausible given
their established competence and backstory. "Young and snarky" is not a
humanizing default; it is the absence of one.

**5.3 A single narrator voice recurs across different protagonists and books.**
Documented: critics observed that a later novel's protagonist had a "snarky,
hipshot, goofy voice" that was "much the same as" the previous novel's
protagonist, and that a female protagonist "happens to think, talk and act like
a geeky 45-year-old" — an outlook "overly manufactured" that undermined the
humour. The influence was described as "following a template" of an ultra-nerdy
narrator across separate books. [Sp §6, critical reviews — Appendix A.3 (iii)]
Guardrail: Gate 9 — a masked-name test on comic-exposition dialogue, run across
named characters within a book and, at series level, across protagonists of
different books.

**5.4 Good intentions function as narrative acquittal for atrocity.**
Documented: a widely cited scholarly essay (*Foundation: The International
Review of Science Fiction*, Vol. 33, No. 90, Spring 2004) argues that the
influence's fiction constructs an "innocent killer" — a character guilty of
tremendous violence, including genocide, who is nonetheless treated as morally
unculpable because his intentions were pure — when "in the real world there is
no such thing as an innocent killer." [Ch §2, scholarly criticism — Appendix
A.3 (iv)] Guardrail: Gate 4 — an action justified by good intention must still
show an on-page consequence that exists independent of the stated purity of
that intention.

**5.5 Didactic sympathy-demand undercuts the good-versus-good premise.**
Documented: scholarship on the influence's moral framework notes a "morality of
intention" — judging characters by who they are and what they meant rather than
by what they did — and observes that this can ask readers for automatic
sympathy with a protagonist regardless of the consequences of his actions,
tipping good-versus-good into one-sided rhetoric rather than a dramatized
dilemma. [Ch §2, scholarly criticism — Appendix A.3 (v)] Guardrail: Gate 2,
plus a dramatization requirement — the opposing good's strongest proponent gets
an on-page scene showing their reasoning in action, never a summarized or
other-character-reported position.

**5.6 Worldbuilding and exposition crowd out tension and stall open threads.**
Documented: reviews of the influence's own science-fiction series identified
"lack of tension" as a book's "biggest flaw" — "rarely did I feel like [the
protagonist] was in actual danger, so some moments that were supposed to be big
deals did not land" — while the book leaned on "higher sci-fi concepts to
explain the growth of these characters," and several story arcs from earlier
books were "set aside" rather than advanced. [St §8–9, reviews — Appendix A.3
(vi)] Guardrail: Gate 13, plus a thread-motion check derived from §1.3 — every
open MICE thread shows forward motion in any book or arc where new
worldbuilding is introduced. Worldbuilding volume is not a substitute for
thread progress.

## 6. The Docking Interface — What a Book Arc Must Supply

This charter is the engine. A book docks into it by supplying five artifacts.
Drafting may not begin until all five exist and pass their format checks.

### 6.0 The Speculative Allowance Table

Where the fantasy charter classifies every capability as hard or soft magic
with a limitation/weakness/cost entry, this charter classifies every technical
or scientific claim into one of three allowance tiers. This is the direct
application of §2.2a, and it is the spine of the whole docking interface.

| Tier | Definition | Reader-legibility burden |
|---|---|---|
| **Established Fact** | Real, verifiable physics, biology, chemistry, or engineering as understood today | Correctness citation in the bible only; no in-world explanation debt |
| **Plausible Extrapolation** | A specified, named extension of established science along a stated vector | Must name the extrapolation rule AND its boundary — the point past which it stops being plausible and becomes invention |
| **Invented Premise** | Purely fictional: FTL, exotic physics, alien biology with no terrestrial analogue | Full fairness-law treatment per §3.1 — a reader-legible rule before it resolves any problem, plus a stated limitation, weakness, or cost by its second on-page use |

Every named technology, phenomenon, alien capability, and institution in the
universe bible carries a tier tag. The tag determines how much explanation debt
the manuscript owes the reader before that element may be used to RESOLVE — not
merely to create — a problem. An untagged element may not resolve anything.

### 6.1 Required artifacts

1. **Universe bible.** The canon layer: the speculative allowance table (§6.0)
   for every named element; for each Invented Premise, its
   limitation/weakness/cost entry (§3.1b); a discovery timeline recording what
   is known, by whom, and when; and the institutional-consequence table showing
   how each element intersects economics, law, labour, or belief (§3.2a).
2. **Arc document.** The living roadmap (§3.3): the locked commitments — ending
   problem, decisive choice, reveal order (§3.3a); per-act promise inventory;
   the moral-cost stake and its checkpoints (§1.2b–c); the MICE thread registry
   (§1.3); and the plant→payoff map covering BOTH speculative reveals and moral
   consequences as ONE map, per §4.3. Departures from the route are logged as
   departed-and-returned, never silently absorbed.
3. **Chapter cards.** One per chapter, each carrying: the opening promise in one
   sentence; the owed delivery type (payoff | progress); MICE thread tags opened
   or closed; any Invented-Premise usage citing its bible constraint entry; and,
   for every new technical explanation included, a one-line answer to the
   Selective Surfacing Test (§2.2b) — which prediction, choice, repair, or
   relationship does this explanation change?
4. **Name registry.** Every named entity, checked at creation for aural
   distinctness from all existing entries (§7.1) and for meaning that does not
   depend on typography. Additionally: every named technology, institution, and
   alien entity is tagged with its allowance tier, so the drafting agent and the
   editor always know which elements are load-bearing real science and which are
   invented premise — independent of whether that distinction is ever surfaced
   to the reader.
5. **State ledger.** The single running record of: open MICE threads by type;
   the moral-cost stake's current condition; the plant/payoff tracker covering
   science reveals and ethical consequences together (one ledger, per §4.3); a
   consequence log for every Invented-Premise usage, answering whether its
   stated cost or limitation has been paid on the page yet; the
   relationship-context log for §1.1c; an interiority-checkpoint log tracking
   whether each act contains a beat establishing character stake independent of
   the technical problem (§5.1); and the phrase-frequency counts for tic
   enforcement (§7.2), technical vocabulary included.

### 6.2 Dock checks
A chapter may be drafted only when: its card exists; every Invented-Premise
element it uses appears in the bible with its allowance tier and constraint
entry; every major character appearing has an independent agenda on record
(§1.1a); the state ledger is current through the previous chapter.

### 6.3 One ledger, many laws
The state ledger deliberately serves multiple gates at once — a single
Invented-Premise consequence entry satisfies both the constraint check (§3.1b)
and the residue check (§1.2c) when the cost is a moral one, per §4.7. It is
append-only during drafting; corrections are new entries, not edits, so the
editor can always reconstruct what the author believed at drafting time. That
reconstruction is what makes §3.4c's exit criteria auditable rather than
asserted.

### 6.4 Precedence
When rules conflict, resolve in this order — higher wins:

    canon (universe bible) > this charter > arc document > chapter card > instinct

A lower layer may never silently override a higher one. If the charter is wrong
for a book, the arc document records an explicit, justified exception — which
is a revision request against the charter, not a local workaround.

## 7. Process Law — From Pipeline Experience

These rules come from production experience with the audio-first pipeline, not
from the brief. They are genre-agnostic and bind all drafting regardless of
pillar. They are carried forward from `THE_AUTHOR.md` §8 with two narrow
science-fiction extensions, each flagged where it appears.

### 7.1 Audio-first rules
1. **Aurally distinct names.** No two named entities in a book may be
   confusable by ear — shared stress pattern plus shared initial phoneme plus
   similar syllable count is a collision. The name-registry check is performed
   aloud, not on paper. Science fiction raises the stakes here: designation
   strings, ship names, and alien nomenclature collide by ear far more readily
   than personal names do.
2. **No typography-carried meaning.** Italics, capitalization, brackets,
   subscripts, units, and font shifts do not survive narration. Any meaning a
   reader would take from typography must also be carried by the words.
3. **Readouts must read aloud.** *(SF extension of the original system-text
   rule.)* Every computer readout, mission-telemetry block, sensor log, and
   AI-companion utterance is drafted to be spoken: no tables-as-prose, no symbol
   soup, no layout-dependent parsing, no equations that only parse on a page. If
   the narrator cannot perform it, it fails. This extension is pipeline-derived,
   not brief-derived, and is stated here as a mandate because the failure it
   prevents is total: an unreadable readout stops a recording session dead.
4. **Never end a chapter file with authoring metadata.** A narrator once read
   "End of Chapter 4, approximately 4,160 words" into a shipped take. Word
   counts, draft notes, and card references live in the card and the ledger —
   never in the manuscript file.

### 7.2 Anti-tic law
1. Any somatic phrase, beat phrase, or explanatory catchphrase used more than
   3 times per book is a defect. The count is grep-able from the state ledger's
   phrase-frequency table.
2. **Technical vocabulary is in scope.** *(SF extension.)* A jargon term or
   stock explanatory formulation repeated identically is the same defect as a
   repeated somatic beat, and is counted in the same table. Precision is not a
   licence for repetition.
3. Replacing a tic with a new repeated phrase is the same defect, not a fix.
   The count transfers to the replacement.
4. **A tic is not a motif.** Repetition that does nameable work — a planted
   echo, a character's signature the plot will pay off, a deliberate refrain
   with a stated function in the arc document — is protected. The test is
   whether the repetition's job can be named in one sentence and is recorded in
   the arc document BEFORE the third use. Unnamed repetition is a tic.

### 7.3 Scene-vs-summary law
1. "Heard about it later" is banned on arc-major beats. Any beat that changes an
   arc-level promise, a relationship's direction, a world rule, a locked
   commitment, or the moral-cost stake happens on the page, in scene.
2. Rationale from production: this pattern once left a book at 43% of target
   density — summary is cheaper to draft and catastrophically cheaper to read.
3. In this genre the law has a specific edge: a technical failure reported after
   the fact is not a speculative consequence, it is a bulletin. Consequence
   chains (§2.1c) are dramatized, not summarized.

### 7.4 Movement and reader law (1.1.1 pipeline extension)

1. The normal drafting unit is a contiguous movement of three to five chapters,
   default four. Full editing follows the movement so causal and emotional joins
   can be judged in context. Between chapters only blockers and ledger state are
   closed.
2. Across twenty chapters, schedule seven or eight embodied pressure set pieces,
   with five as the hard floor and no gap longer than four chapters. An EVA,
   chase, rescue, dangerous test, failure cascade, repair under a clock,
   survival sequence, confrontation, or combat qualifies when it forces action
   and irreversible choice.
3. Minor pressure sequences normally receive 600–1,000 words; major sequences
   1,500–2,500; climaxes may span scenes or chapters. These bands protect scene
   function from summary and do not reward padding.
4. Across two ordinary chapters, aim for four organic humor or warmth
   opportunities, with three to five as the working band. A declared cold
   movement may carry less. This is a rolling rhythm, not a chapter quota.
5. Reader A reads every movement. Reader B reads the opening, act turns, ending,
   and flagged movements. Scores are trends; located confusion, disbelief,
   skimming, or delight controls repair.

## 8. The Editor's Unified Gate

One deduplicated list, derived from the three pillars. Every gate is binary,
checkable against the draft without reference to author intent, and traceable
to its source principle. Run per chapter unless marked per-arc or per-book.

Twenty-one gates: sixteen research-derived craft gates plus five
pipeline-derived reader gates. The latter enforce observed production needs and
are labelled as such; they do not claim research provenance.

**Character and moral gates (Pillar I — Ch)**

1. **Relational character.** Every major character's want is statable
   independent of the protagonist's goal, AND the character shows measurably
   different behaviour across ≥2 relationship contexts. Either clause failing
   = FAIL. [Ch §1.1a–b; guards §5.2]
2. **Two live goods.** Every major moral-choice scene names two goods in
   conflict, and the opposing good's strongest proponent gets an on-page
   dramatized scene showing their reasoning in action. One option secretly
   villainous, or the opposing case delivered only in summary or by report =
   FAIL. [Ch §1.2a; guards §5.5]
3. **Priced and referenced choice.** Every major moral choice costs something
   specific and named, AND that cost is referenced again later in the book or
   series. A vague cost, or a cost never mentioned again = FAIL. [Ch §1.2b–c]
4. **Intention-consequence split.** An action justified by good intention still
   shows an on-page consequence that exists independent of that intention.
   Consequence waived because the motive was pure = FAIL. [Ch §1.2d; guards
   §5.4]
5. **MICE type-match closure.** Every thread's closing type matches its opening
   type's owed discipline; nested threads close on their own terms. Mismatch,
   or an inner thread silently discharged by an outer closure = FAIL. [Ch
   §1.3a–c]

**Speculative-method gates (Pillar II — Sp)**

6. **Speculative origin traceability.** Every plot problem traces back to one of
   the declared one-or-two core speculative changes. A problem imported from an
   unrelated, untethered new wonder = FAIL. [Sp §2.1a–b]
7. **Allowance ledger and selective surfacing.** Every technical claim appears
   in the bible tagged Established Fact / Plausible Extrapolation / Invented
   Premise, AND every on-page technical explanation changes a prediction, a
   choice, a repair, or a relationship. Untagged claim, or an explanation
   serving none of the four = FAIL. [Sp §2.2a–b]
8. **Humour vehicle, no missing step.** Comic exposition is delivered through
   humour, teaching, frustration, or interpersonal asymmetry, and no causal
   science step is skipped and covered by a joke. A joke standing in place of a
   causal link, or a block of exposition with no character-voiced pressure
   behind it = FAIL. [Sp §2.3a–b]
9. **Voice distinctness under humour.** Masked-name test: two named characters'
   comic-exposition dialogue is not interchangeable; at series level, two
   books' protagonists are not interchangeable either. Indistinguishable =
   FAIL. [Sp §2.3c; guards §5.3]
10. **Interiority independent of problem.** Every major sequence carries ≥1
    beat establishing a character stake that is not itself the technical
    problem. A character whose only stated interior state IS the science
    problem = FAIL. [Sp §2.3c applied; guards §5.1–5.2]

**World-law, structure, and process gates (Pillar III — St)**

11. **Solution traceability and constraint tagging.** Every resolution by
    device, institution, biology, or speculative-physics rule cites the earlier
    passage establishing that rule, AND every named Invented-Premise capability
    with ≥2 on-page uses states a limitation, weakness, or cost by its second
    use. Either missing = FAIL. [St §3.1a–b]
12. **Extend-before-invent ratio.** Per act: new named technologies or
    institutions introduced may not exceed existing ones extended by more than
    2:1. Ratio exceeded = FAIL. [St §3.1c]
13. **Six-function worldbuilding test.** Worldbuilding passages over 150 words
    change at least one of access, danger, belief, labour, relationship, or
    choice for a named character; and every open MICE thread shows forward
    motion in any arc where new worldbuilding is introduced. None applying, or
    threads static under new worldbuilding = FAIL. [St §3.2a; guards §5.6]
14. **Locked-commitment integrity** (per arc). The ending problem, the decisive
    choice, and the reveal order match what was locked before drafting began. A
    locked commitment changed without a logged, justified exception in the arc
    document = FAIL. [St §3.3a–b]
15. **Foreshadow before reveal.** Every major reveal — speculative or moral —
    has ≥1 earlier plant with a lower chapter number. Absent = FAIL. [St
    §3.3c; §4.2]
16. **Bounded revision** (per book). Any large revision pass names the specific
    structural failure it addresses, AND does not reopen a section whose
    declared exit criteria already passed without a newly logged failure.
    Either clause failing = FAIL. [St §3.4a, §3.4c; scoped by §4.6]

**Reader and movement gates (pipeline-derived — §7.4)**

17. **Opening pressure.** Within the first 150 words, a concrete discrepancy,
    danger, want, or consequential question matters to a character now. General
    orientation without present pressure = FAIL. [Pipeline §7.4]
18. **Problem/action cadence** (per book and movement). The problem engine
    advances in every chapter; each movement carries an inconvenient result,
    meaningful revision, and changed choice. The book schedules seven or eight
    embodied pressure set pieces per twenty chapters, never fewer than five and
    never more than four chapters apart. An unexplained gap = FAIL. [Pipeline
    §7.4]
19. **Set-piece sustain.** A scheduled MINOR pressure sequence normally sustains
    600–1,000 words; MAJOR 1,500–2,500; CLIMAX carries escalation, choice, cost,
    and aftermath even when it crosses chapters. Compression that turns the
    event into summary, or padding without function = FAIL. [Pipeline §7.4;
    Process Law §7.3]
20. **Rolling humor and warmth.** Across two ordinary chapters, aim for four
    organic opportunities, with three to five acceptable. A declared cold
    movement is exempt. Treating this as a per-chapter joke quota, or sustained
    emotional airlessness without a declared purpose = FAIL. [Pipeline §7.4]
21. **Cold-reader continuity and dialogue ownership.** Located reader confusion,
    disbelief, skimming, or lost desire to continue must be resolved or
    explicitly held; score changes are interpreted as trends. Masked dialogue
    samples only substantive lines of at least eight words or lines carrying a
    position, decision, evasion, or conflict. Ignoring located experience or
    manufacturing quirks from trivial samples = FAIL. [Pipeline §7.4]

*(Process Law §7 adds its own checks, all grep-able against the manuscript and
ledger rather than adjudicated per chapter: name-registry aural collisions,
typography-carried meaning, unspeakable readouts, phrase and jargon counts
above 3, terminal authoring metadata, and summary on arc-major beats. The
living arc document required by §3.3 is enforced structurally by the Docking
Interface §6.1.2 rather than as a per-chapter gate.)*
