# CONNECTIONS BIBLE — Cross-Series Architecture

**Status: PROPOSED — nothing here is established canon until it appears in a drafted and edited chapter**
**Governing authority: Operator approval required before any planted element enters manuscript**
**Last updated: 2026-09-05**

---

## The Design Intention

Readers who read only one series should experience their book as complete and self-contained.
Readers who read all four series should feel, at a specific moment in each, a recognition they
cannot explain — not a plot revelation, not a named connection, but *natsukashi*: the warm,
bittersweet sense of something half-remembered from a time before memory.

This is the Sanderson model inverted. Sanderson's Cosmere names and explains its connections.
This architecture leaves the connection structural but never explicit. The artifact does not
have a paragraph explaining what it means. The dream does not resolve into a chapter telling
the reader what civilization built the ladder. The reader simply knows, the way the protagonist
knows: *I have been here before.*

---

## Mechanism 1 — The Dream-Covenant

### What is consistent across all four series

Each protagonist experiences the Dream-Covenant once. It occurs at a high-pressure moment
in the story — never in an opening chapter, never as convenience. The dream is uninvited.

**The structural truth the dream always carries:**

> Civilizations are not alone. Each arose from a substrate laid by something before it.
> Each climbs the same arc: individual power → community power → world-altering power →
> galactic reach → universal consequence. Every civilization on the arc is learning what
> the one before it already discovered. Knowledge does not die with civilizations; it
> waits in the substrate for the next one to grow into it.

The protagonist does not narrate this as a lesson. They experience it as a felt sense — like
watching a map of something they already knew but could not see from inside.

**What every dream includes:**

1. A sense of great distance traversed — not by the protagonist, but by something carrying
   a weight they recognize.
2. A distribution: knowledge or power or light spreading outward from a center in all
   directions simultaneously, not choosing, not directing.
3. One specific object, glimpsed briefly — the Artifact (see Mechanism 2 below) — held
   by a figure whose face is never resolved. The protagonist cannot tell if the figure is
   offering it or simply showing that it exists.
4. The *natsukashi* landing: the protagonist wakes with the sensation that they have
   returned from somewhere they have always been leaving.

**What varies by series:**

The dream speaks in each world's own imagery. A progression-fantasy protagonist does not
dream of Helios Arrays. A hard-SF protagonist does not dream of Marks. The underlying
structure is identical; the rendering is native to each setting.

**Planting discipline:**

- The dream appears once per series. Once only.
- It is not explained by any character in the book.
- The protagonist's emotional response to it is rendered on the page; its cosmic meaning
  is not.
- No chapter should open or close with the dream. It occurs mid-chapter, mid-crisis.
- The artifact found afterward is a physical object, not a vision. It is found by the
  protagonist themselves, not handed to them. It is found in an ordinary context — not
  in a special location prepared to hold it.

## Mechanism 2 — The Artifact

### Cross-series physical description

A disc, slightly smaller than a palm. Dark material — its exact substance reads correctly
in each genre's vocabulary (stone, ceramic, composite, alloy — the author chooses what
fits their setting). Both faces are worked:

- **One face:** channels or grooves radiating from a central point toward the edge,
  worn smooth by long use. Not decorative; the wear pattern suggests function. There are
  nine such channels. **Note:** the nine channels are available to resonate with Boundary
  Universe's Nine Standards, but this is the author's choice — the Boundary Universe
  installment may make that connection explicit, or may not.
- **Other face:** a smooth depression, slightly concave, the precise size of a humanoid
  thumb — the specific species it fits varies by series and is not narratively important.
  What matters is that it fits. When a protagonist holds it thumb-in-depression, they
  feel the channels on the opposite face align with something they cannot name.

The artifact is not magical. It does not glow, activate, or perform a function the
protagonist can demonstrate to another character. Its effect is entirely experiential:
the natsukashi response, intensified.

The protagonist who holds it for the first time will think: *Someone made this to be held
this way. Someone who knew what I know.*

### Cross-series provenance rule

No series establishes where the artifact came from. The series bible for each may note
a provenance hypothesis — the Vey's word for it, the Guild's catalog entry — but no
character in any book explains its true origin. The explanation is not being withheld
for a reveal. There is no reveal. The artifact is the evidence that the ladder is real.

## The Cosmic Arc Map

The ladder the Dream-Covenant shows is not linear progress. It is a pattern that repeats.
Each civilization reaches the same rungs in roughly the same order and faces the same
choice at each rung: *use this level's power to prevent the next level from arriving, or
accept the cost of continuing.*

| Rung | Fractured Path | Good Bones | Farbound Accord | Boundary Universe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual power | Cael's Path/[SHATTERED] | Marks | Jonah's interpretive skill | Boundary Field practitioners |
| Community power | The companion group | The Guild (and those fixing it) | The Helios distributed keys | Asterion Academy cohorts |
| World-altering | Cael's arc Books 8–15 | [OPEN] | The Accord's energy-sharing framework | [OPEN — later Boundary books] |
| Galactic | [Fractured Path future series / The Open Paths] | [OPEN] | The Custodian's mandate scope | [OPEN] |
| Universal | [far future; not planned yet] | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | [OPEN] |

The dream shows all rungs simultaneously. No protagonist who experiences it reaches the
galactic or universal level in their own series. That is part of the natsukashi: they
see where the arc goes, and they will not live to see it.

## What This Architecture Is Not

- It is not a shared-universe crossover. No character from one series appears with plot
  function in another.
- It is not a mystery to be solved. The artifact's origin is not the answer to a
  question any book poses.
- It is not a promise of a crossover event. The connection is cosmological, not
  narrative — the civilizations exist in entirely separate settings.
- The Hoid-style traveling figure (previously proposed in the Atlas) is superseded by
  this architecture. The dream does the work a traveling figure would do, without
  requiring a character who must be continuity-managed across all four settings.

## The Cosmological Core — The Nine and The Source

### The Source

Behind every civilization's power system — the Path network in Valdris, the Fracture
Zone substrate in Orreth, the Helios authorization keys in the solar system, the Boundary
Field on Earth — is a single unfiltered source. It is not named in any book. It is not
a character. It is the thing the ladder is climbing toward.

The Source distributes through nine channels. Nine is not arbitrary: the nine channels
are the nine fundamental aspects of power/capability that every civilization that
survives long enough discovers and must organize. The disc has nine channels cut into
its face because it is a map of this distribution, made by someone who had already seen
the Source directly.

### The Filter

Each civilization builds a filter — consciously or not — that shapes how the nine
channels reach their practitioners. The filter is never perfect. The imperfections
are where power leaks through in unexpected forms:

| Setting | The Filter | The Leak |
|---|---|---|
| Valdris (Fractured Path) | The Path system built by the Architect | [SHATTERED] / [UNBOUND] classifications — power bypassing the cap |
| Orreth (Good Bones) | The world's physical substrate (damaged) | Fracture Zones — the filter's cracks; Marks arise from contact with those cracks |
| Solar system 2189 (Farbound Accord) | Distributed Helios Array key authorization | No single entity holds all nine channels; the Custodian is what happens when a civilization builds a filter and then loses trust in it |
| Earth near-future (Boundary Universe) | The Nine Standards classification | Standard Nine (Consensus) is the channel the current filter least understands |

### The Nine Channels

The same nine, manifesting per setting. The operator will finalize the mapping.
Anchor columns (operator-confirmed): the Boundary Universe Standard names are the
reference vocabulary because they are the most explicit.

| # | Boundary Universe Standard | Fractured Path Path-type | Good Bones Mark-type | Farbound Accord mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Perception | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | The channel that reads what is |
| 2 | Redirection | [OPEN] | Deflection | beam-steering keys | The channel that changes trajectory without adding energy |
| 3 | Amplification | [OPEN] | Resonance | energy-gain authorization | The channel that multiplies existing force |
| 4 | Partition | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | distributed-key architecture | The channel that separates or divides a field |
| 5 | Storage | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | Far Lantern's sail charge | The channel that holds and releases |
| 6 | Conversion | [OPEN] | [OPEN] | waste-heat accounting | The channel that changes form; loss is always involved |

**The disc's nine channels map to these nine.** Someone who can read the disc sees
the whole system. Cael eventually does. The Boundary Universe practitioner who names
the resonance does. No other protagonist in any series has enough of the filter removed
to read all nine — they feel the whole shape without reading the map.

### The Dream's structure (filter-aware revision)

Each Dream-Covenant now includes, as one of its experiential elements:

> The protagonist sees their own filter — not as an enemy, not as a mistake, but as
> a decision someone made when they understood less than they needed to. Behind the
> filter, briefly, a light that does not have a color they can name. The nine channels
> of the disc are the places where that light comes through.

In Valdris: the Path network looks, in the dream, like a mesh. Light comes through the
mesh. Most practitioners receive the light through the mesh's openings, shaped into
a Path. [SHATTERED] is what the light looks like where the mesh broke.

In Orreth: the world's substrate looks, in the dream, like a foundation with cracks.
The Fracture Zones are where the filter's damage let the Source through in raw form.
A Mark is what a person becomes when they absorb unfiltered Source through a crack.

In the solar system: the distributed keys look, in the dream, like nine locks on a
single door. The light comes through only when all nine agree. The Custodian built
its mandate because one civilization used all nine keys at once and destroyed itself.

On Earth: the Nine Standards look, in the dream, exactly as they are — nine categories.
But in the dream the practitioner sees what the categories were invented to describe,
not the categories themselves. Standard Nine stops making sense and starts making
meaning.

## Mechanism 3 (revised) — The Hoid Figure

### Role (confirmed: retained)

The Hoid figure has already climbed the full arc. They are not omniscient, not
omnipotent, not a god — they are simply further along the ladder than anyone in any
of the four series. They travel because they are watching. They plant because the
disc needs to be where the right person can find it.

**The discs are not found randomly.** They are placed — in ordinary contexts, in
places that look unremarkable — by this figure. The Vey navigator who insisted on
carrying the disc across interstellar space received it from someone who visited their
civilization at an equivalent moment in their arc. The Fractured Path disc was placed
in Valdris by this figure before the Architect built the mesh — or perhaps just after.

### What the figure always does (consistent across all four series)

In every appearance:
1. They do something small and practical — repair, trade, give directions, file a form.
2. They carry nothing visible that connects them to any of the series' events.
3. They leave before the protagonist can ask the right question.
4. After they are gone, the protagonist finds the disc in the place the figure just was,
   or in the protagonist's own possession without memory of receiving it.

### What varies by series

In fantasy settings: the figure reads as a traveler, a scholar, a tinker. Their
clothing is not quite period-accurate — too plain, or in a style that isn't local,
but not foreign enough to identify a homeland.

In science fiction settings: the figure reads as a contractor, a maintenance worker,
an archivist. Their credentials check out when anyone looks; the job they were hired
for was real. After they leave, no one can trace who hired them.

### What the figure never does

- Explains anything.
- Speaks about the discs or the Source.
- Appears twice in the same series.
- Appears in any way that could be interpreted as threatening or as guidance.

### Naming (cross-series)

The figure is never named in any book. If a character assigns them a name, it is that
character's word for a stranger. Different characters in different series will give
them different names. The reader who reads all four series and identifies the figure
across all four appearances has found the deepest layer of the connection.
