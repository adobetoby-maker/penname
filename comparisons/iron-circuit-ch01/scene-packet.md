# Scene Packet — Iron Circuit, Chapter 1 "Rated"

**Facts only. No prose from any prior draft appears in this card.**

Target length: 4,200–4,800 words.
POV: close third, Cael. Past tense.
Primary job: FIRST DRAFT.

---

## Situation

Cael is 15. He has lived in Ardenmere just over one year. It is evening at the
Ironyard, an old converted warehouse at the eastern edge of the Unranked
District — three fighting spaces, low ceilings, practitioner-rigged lighting,
stone floor worn smooth, smells of impact dust and iron fittings and lamp oil.

He has just won a circuit bout. He is in a side alcove by the east wall where
practitioners habitually go to write up a fight while memory is fresh.

## What happened in the bout (already over when the chapter opens)

- Opponent: Ulric. Copper-tier formal, Blade Path, six years guild training.
  Technically Cael's superior in formal ability and his senior in experience.
- Cael watched four of Ulric's previous bouts — roughly six hours — before
  agreeing to terms. This is now his standard procedure.
- Ulric's tell: a shoulder-drop that telegraphs a committed strike. Cael first
  spotted it in the second watched bout and confirmed it across the rest.
- Second exchange: Ulric caught Cael clean, low and left, behind a high feint.
  Cael was watching hands instead of weight — his own lapse, an old bad habit.
  Impact to the thigh. The leg still holds but dislikes stairs.
- Fifth exchange: Cael used the Wind-adjacent fragment, evaded the committed
  strike, took position on Ulric's open side, and won.

## Cael's method (established, not new)

- Watch-count rule: one instance is accident, two coincidence, three structure.
  He does not build a plan on fewer than three. He broke this once, in spring,
  against bait, and lost. That loss is underlined in the notebook.
- Six hours of watching, divided by layer: bout one rhythm, bout two weight,
  bout three breath and face, bout four everything at once.
- Cost-to-fake principle: hands are cheap to fake, weight is expensive, breath
  under load is nearly impossible.
- Notebook uses three columns: confirmed, probable, discarded.

## The two fragments (his own anomalous capabilities)

**Wind-adjacent** — evasion, short burst. Reliable; he trusts it completely.
Oddity worth noting: it arrives *more* cleanly the worse the moment is, unlike
every other skill he owns, which degrade under stress. Costs:
- deploys on a held half-breath; he cannot breathe during it
- a locked landing beat at the end — a half-breath where he cannot change
  direction. Currently exploitable by anyone who identifies it.
- hip strain, leading side, cumulative. One burst free; three means a limp.

**Pressure-adjacent** — gathering density in forearm and shoulder; lets him
absorb a hit and stay planted. Data incomplete. Costs: slow to gather (needs
most of a full breath, so it must be planned not reacted), drains roughly three
times a Wind burst, and it *telegraphs* — his weight sinks and stance widens,
announcing that he has chosen to receive rather than evade.

Fragment log: two entries. Recently separated into its own slim volume from the
observation notebook, which has twenty-three entries.

## People

- **Vell** — head Ledger-keeper, 50s, retired Bronze-tier who surrendered guild
  credentials in a certification dispute. Meticulous, fair, dry. Logs Cael's
  result as "Assessed-Copper, win, atypical movement pattern." She has stopped
  asking him to explain the atypical part. She warns him, without heat, that
  he has beaten eleven Copper-tier formals this year and some of them will stop
  being gracious about it.
- **Lira** — his training partner and closest person. Comes to find him after
  her own session. She designs training combinations to expose gaps and refuses
  to tell him what she is testing, because a warning changes how he moves. She
  is the one who discovered the landing beat by watching from outside — he
  could not have found it himself. She has read the fragment log more than
  anyone but him.
- **Dace** — Circuit Master, not a practitioner, takes his share from the door
  rather than the purse, so he has no stake in outcomes.
- **A dried-fruit vendor** in the market — older woman, sells to him weekly,
  asks after whatever he is limping on.

## Economics (use only what the scene needs)

Twelve silver marks posted for the bout: eight to the winner, three to the
loser, one to the ledger. The loser's share exists so beaten fighters keep
coming back instead of getting desperate. Rent is nine marks a month for their
two rooms at a practitioners' boarding house two streets from the Ironyard.

## Obligations this chapter must discharge

1. Establish the circuit as **daily life**, not as setting being toured.
2. Cael's rating is Assessed-Copper; his method is watching, and it works.
3. Both fragments are on the page with their costs legible.
4. The Lira relationship reads as mutual and load-bearing, not as support staff.
5. Land the fact that Lira found the landing beat — he does not have an outside
   view of himself and she does.
6. End on changed pressure, not on a summary of the chapter.
