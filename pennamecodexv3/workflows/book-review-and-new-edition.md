# Monroe Jackson 1.1.1 — Book Review and New Edition Loop

This is the standard improvement process for an existing Monroe Jackson book.
Its purpose is to make a good book more exciting, connected, and readable—not
to replace its voice or manufacture endless revision.

## 1. Intake and freeze

1. Assign the genre seat and canonical byline.
2. Copy `templates/AUTHORSHIP.template.md` and record standard `1.1.1`.
3. Preserve the current manuscript as the source edition with a commit or tag.
4. Inventory chapters, canon, arc, state, registry, and unresolved series
   obligations. Missing material becomes an explicit gap, not invented canon.
5. State the book's reader promise and the reason for this edition in one
   sentence each. “Make it better” is not a review target.

## 2. Read before editing

Read the complete source edition without changing prose. Review it in contiguous
movements of three to five chapters and maintain one rolling excitement map.

- Reader A reads every movement for clarity, interest, confusion, and desire to
  continue.
- Reader B reads the opening, act turns, climax/ending, and every movement
  flagged by A.
- The genre specialist checks scheduled action and power/cost in fantasy, or
  problem/action, technical causality, discovery, and consequence in science
  fiction.
- Scores show trends. Located moments of confusion, skimming, disbelief, or
  delight govern decisions.

Do not repair while discovering. Finish the diagnosis so a late book-level
cause is not repeatedly patched as ten local symptoms.

## 3. Produce the entitled review

Create `BOOK REVIEW AND NEW EDITION — [TITLE]` from
`templates/BOOK_REVIEW_REPORT.template.md`. It must answer:

1. Would a fresh reader keep reading and buy the next book?
2. Where does excitement rise, flatten, or break?
3. Which strengths are responsible for the book already working?
4. Which located problems have a real reader consequence?
5. What is the smallest repair that changes that consequence?

Give every chapter one disposition: `KEEP`, `TIGHTEN`, `REPAIR`,
`RESTRUCTURE`, `INSERT`, or `CUT`. Protect working scenes explicitly. A global
rewrite requires a named structural failure supported across the book.

## 4. Build the new edition in movements

Revise three to five connected chapters at a time, normally four. Resolve
book-level dependencies first, then movement seams, then chapter problems.

1. Give the correct Monroe Jackson author seat one consolidated repair brief.
2. The author repairs in reading order and preserves named strengths.
3. A cross-family editor reads the whole changed movement, including its joins.
4. Reader A re-reads every changed movement; reader B re-reads the opening, act
   turns, ending, and flagged movements.
5. Run the fantasy fight audit or science-fiction problem/action audit only
   where scheduled.
6. Consolidate findings into one repair pass. Additions may total up to eight
   percent of a chapter target across cycles before architecture must be
   reconsidered.
7. Recheck only prior findings and changed joins. Two repair cycles is the
   normal ceiling; a third surviving cycle returns the problem to architecture.

## 5. Read the book that now exists

After all movements close, run four separate whole-book passes in order:

1. **Structural continuity:** causal chain, chronology, state, plants/payoffs,
   relationship movement, escalation, climax, aftermath, and series handoff.
2. **Voice and character:** Monroe Jackson seat identity, distinguishable
   dialogue, emotional truth, humor distribution, and protected strengths.
3. **Line edit:** clarity, repetition, tics, grammar, and paragraph rhythm. Do
   not reopen structure without a newly logged failure.
4. **Listening proof:** hear the complete book; repair names, readouts, system
   text, sentence overload, and any meaning carried only by typography.

## 6. Lock the edition

Compare changed passages with their source only against the declared reader
consequence. Close all HIGH/BLOCKER findings, complete the authorship record,
update canon/state/registry, and tag or commit the new edition. Keep the frozen
source recoverable.

The loop ends at `LOCKED`. New taste notes do not reopen a completed edition
without a new, located reader failure.

## Catalogue rollout

For a catalogue of roughly ten books, pilot this on one fantasy and one science
fiction title first. Calibrate reader reports and repair size, then move through
the queue in small waves. Each book keeps its own report, source tag, author
record, and exit checks; findings never leak from one title into another by
assumption.

For a twenty-chapter book, the planning shape is usually five movements, five
batch edits, five Reader A passes, three to five Reader B passes, only the
scheduled specialist audits, three to five consolidated repair/rechecks, and
four whole-book passes. These are estimates, not quotas.
