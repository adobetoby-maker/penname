The third bridge stone shifted under Iven's heel.

Mara saw the opening first: his weight stranded beyond the seam, one palm
pressed to stone he could no longer trust. One clean shove would put him across
the boundary and give her the point.

Then the seam widened. It ran too straight across the grain.

Mara released the strike and caught his wrist. Pain opened through her injured
hand. Her fingers would not close, so she hooked her forearm beneath his and
dropped her weight backward. The broken segment fell away before Iven did.

The steward called the pause. Iven looked from the missing stone to the distance
Mara had surrendered, then to the hand she could no longer hide against her
chest.

“You had the point,” he said.

Mara crouched at the surviving edge. A pale diagonal cut crossed the dark break,
cleaner than any natural fracture.

“We have a different problem.”
