# Craft layer — The Iron Prince Expansion

Owner-directed (Kindling, this pass). Not an action-stack layer in the combat
sense — it applies to any scene where a power is called, discovered, or used
in a form the reader has not yet fully learned. Compose it with whatever
action stack the scene already needs (`clean`, `learning`, etc.); it adds
detail requirements, it does not replace the combat discipline in `CORE.md`.

## When this layer is active

- The first time any viewpoint character calls a given power or technique.
- The first time the viewpoint character *witnesses* a given power used by
  someone else, whether or not they can call it themselves — see "The awe
  piece," below. This is a separate trigger from calling it; a character can
  watch a power for the first time long before, or instead of, ever using it.
- Most times after that, for as long as the reader is still learning how the
  power actually works — its cost, its limits, its visible form.
- Any time a power changes: a new technique, a new rank, a new domain, a
  blend that hasn't been shown before. The layer re-activates for the new
  thing even in a book where the character's base power is long since
  routine.
- It retires for a given power once the reader has been shown enough uses
  that the mechanism is fully legible without restating it — at that point,
  a plain reference (per `CORE.md`) is correct and this layer would be inert
  repetition.

## The call

Do not skip from want to result. Name the specific thing the character does
to ask: what they hold, where their attention goes, what it costs to ask
honestly versus flinch-ask under panic. A "call" is a discrete, repeatable
action with its own texture — Toren's early calls are effortful and visibly
uncertain; later calls in the same book should read as the same action grown
faster, not a different action.

## The light is the structure, not a decoration

When a power manifests as visible light, heat, or force, that manifestation
*is* the shape of what the technique actually does — never ornamental glow
laid over an otherwise-invisible mechanic. If the result is a blade, the
light should be seen taking the blade's geometry as it forms, not simply
"glowing" before a blade is already there. Describe the forming shape in the
order the viewpoint character actually perceives it: source, direction,
edges, the moment it commits to a final form.

## Seeing the source

Give the reader something to visualize for the internal reservoir the power
is drawn from — not an abstract "power" but a located, physical-feeling
sensation the viewpoint character can track: a pull, a weight shifting, a
door swinging on a hinge somewhere behind the sternum, cold or warmth moving
a specific direction. This should draw on `CANON.md`'s existing language for
the soul/reservoir rather than invent a competing metaphor. A "small tug" on
first calls should read as genuinely small — save the larger sensations for
larger draws, so the scale stays honest.

## The light-to-ember threshold — a visible mastery marker

Use a consistent, trackable visual vocabulary for a specific competence
threshold: early on, the manifestation only holds while the character's
attention stays on it — a light that needs tending, that gutters if the mind
wanders. Mastery is marked, concretely and specifically, by the moment the
character can look away and it stays lit: the power has stopped being a held
thing and become a standing one. Treat this threshold as a real story beat
with its own weight the first time it happens for a given power, not a
passing description. Once shown, later instances can reference it briefly
rather than re-explain it.

## The awe piece — first time seen, not just first time used

Witnessing is its own beat, distinct from calling. The first time the
viewpoint character sees a power they do not have, or have not yet seen used
this way, give the reader their felt reaction before the tactical read of it:
the half-second where scale, wrongness, or wonder lands ahead of
understanding. Do not skip straight to "and it worked" or to the tactical
consequence. Let the character register that something just happened that
their own model of the world did not have room for, specifically named rather
than a generic "amazing" — what about it was impossible, too fast, too clean,
too quiet, wrong-sized. This is not decoration layered on top of the action;
it is the same discipline as "the light is the structure," aimed at the
witness instead of the caller. A character who has now seen a power once does
not need the awe beat repeated at full weight the second time — a brief
callback to the first time (per the mastery-threshold guidance above) is
usually enough once the reader already has the image.

## Combat choreography density — superseded, now the universal default

As of Monroe 1.3, `KINETIC.md` itself adopts the full Salvatore standard
(named technical vocabulary, blow-by-blow precision, terrain-causality,
per-fighter distinct style, tempo contrast) as the default for every fight in
the book, not an exception this layer had to unlock. There is no more
restrained baseline to escalate past. This layer's own job stays what it was
originally built for: the power-manifestation content above (the call, light-
as-structure, seeing the source, the mastery threshold, the awe piece) — apply
`KINETIC.md` directly for combat choreography, on every fight, not just the
ones this layer touches. The one thing worth carrying forward from this
layer's own drafting into general practice: invent technical vocabulary from
the character's real, established training rather than a costume word list —
Rook's Stride/Ember blend reads differently from a sword-and-shield stylist,
because it is a differently trained body.

## Slow motion for a named master's technique

When an established, skilled practitioner (Rook, or any character whose
technique the reader hasn't fully seen yet) uses their power, slow the
prose's actual tempo, not just the in-story time. Extend the sentence-level
detail: break the movement into its real physical stages, name exactly what
the viewpoint character's eyes catch and in what order, and let small
physical facts (weight transfer, a held breath, where the hands are a half-
second before they matter) carry the beat instead of summarizing the result.
This is not slow motion as a visual-effects cliché — it is giving the reader
enough real, sequential, physical information to reconstruct the technique
themselves, the way the viewpoint character is actually experiencing it
happening faster than they can fully process in the moment, and slower than
that once their trained eye starts sorting it.

## Punctuation and pacing for these passages

Apply the standing punctuation-integrity standard (`scripts/punctuation_review.py`
— word-locked, voice-preserving, not a grammar-correctness pass) with
particular attention inside Iron Prince passages to sentence-ending placement:
let a short, isolated sentence or paragraph carry a reveal or a landed beat
by itself, so the punch is not diluted by trailing clauses after it. This is
a composition choice made while drafting or revising the passage, not a
mechanical rule the punctuation-integrity scan can apply after the fact.

## Four-question check for this layer

1. Could a reader who has never seen this power before sketch what it looked
   like, in order, from the prose alone?
2. Is the cost/source sensation specific and scaled to the size of the draw?
3. If this is a mastery-threshold moment, is the shift (held → standing;
   tended → automatic) shown as a discrete beat, not narrated past?
4. If this is a named master's technique, does the prose give enough
   sequential physical detail that the reader could describe the movement
   back, not just its outcome?
5. If this is the viewpoint character's first time seeing this power used,
   does the reader get the character's felt reaction — specifically named,
   not generic wonder — before the scene moves on to what it means tactically?
