# Module — Hard Science and Problem Solving

## Author guidance

Maintain a premise ledger that distinguishes established science, supported
extrapolation, and explicit invention. Once the book declares its speculative
allowances, treat their consequences consistently.

For each major technical turn, show the operational chain: observation,
hypothesis, test, result, interpretation, and changed choice. Compress routine
work, not the surprising result or the decision it forces.

Technical explanation belongs where it changes action. Use quantities when the
scale affects a decision; do not use arithmetic as decorative authority. Preserve
uncertainty and measurement limits.

Prefer recombination of established tools over convenient new technology. Record
failures, resource consumption, calibration, interfaces, and downstream repairs.

## Editor gates

1. **Premise classification.** Every load-bearing technical claim is supported by
   the declared science, extrapolation, or invention ledger.
2. **Causal chain.** The solution follows from visible evidence and reasoning; no
   missing technical step performs the actual rescue.
3. **Test integrity.** Experiments have an interpretable setup, result, uncertainty,
   and consequence rather than functioning as ceremonial competence.
4. **Constraint persistence.** Time, energy, mass, heat, biology, communication,
   calibration, and resource limits persist until explicitly changed.
5. **Exposition utility.** Technical explanation changes a choice, prediction,
   relationship, or risk in the scene.
6. **Tool provenance.** A decisive device or method was established, tested, or
   plausibly available before its climactic use.
7. **Failure value.** A failed attempt changes the model or options; repeated failure
   without new information is compressed.
