# Module — Moral Choice and Relationship

## Author guidance

Build important choices from competing goods, partial knowledge, and unequal cost.
Before drafting, name what each option protects, who pays, and which uncertainty
cannot be removed in time.

Give every major participant an independent purpose. Character appears through
relationship: the same person behaves differently with someone they need, fear,
teach, resent, command, or love.

Carry moral consequence beyond the decision. A correct outcome does not erase the
method, damaged trust, compromised value, or person who bore the cost.

## Editor gates

1. **Live alternatives.** At least two choices are genuinely available before the
   decision; the rejected option is not made absurd to flatter the protagonist.
2. **Competing value.** Each serious option protects a recognizable good or avoids a
   recognizable harm.
3. **Cost ownership.** The text identifies who pays for the choice, including costs
   displaced onto less powerful characters.
4. **Knowledge honesty.** The chooser acts on information actually available at the
   time; hindsight is not smuggled into the decision.
5. **Relationship specificity.** Major characters pursue independent purposes and
   expose relationship-specific behavior rather than serving as plot functions.
6. **Residue.** Consequences persist into later behavior, trust, obligation, status,
   or self-conception.
7. **No arithmetic absolution.** Technical correctness informs the moral decision but
   does not automatically settle it.
