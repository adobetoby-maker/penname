# Optional Module — Tournament-Arc Progression

Apply only when selected by the scene packet. Owned by `monroe`; available
to any pen name whose manifest entry lists it in `allowed_modules`.

This module is the mechanical layer for a single-lineage training-arc/
tournament-arc structure: an underdog protagonist, a coarse-then-fine
rank/tier scale, a gear-or-system analog whose own growth is gated by paid
combat cost, a rank-ordered sparring ladder running under a formal
tournament, and rivals who keep advancing off page. It is more specific than
`progression.md`; use both together when the scene packet calls for this
exact structure, or `progression.md` alone for progression scenes that do
not involve a ranked ladder or tournament.

## Author guidance

- Keep the protagonist's floor verifiably worst-in-room on every measured
  axis but one. The exception axis is potential, not performance — it
  produces nothing until it is spent, and it is spent by losing, ideally to
  opponents above the protagonist's current tier.
- Score growth on a small set of named axes readers can hold in their head
  (an offense-analog, a defense-analog, an adaptation/growth-analog), on a
  coarse-then-fine scale (a few letter tiers, each with finer sub-ranks).
- Any scene that raises a tracked stat states the specific delta and what
  was paid for it. "Trained and got stronger" with no legible before/after
  and no shown cost is not a progression beat.
- At least once per arc, let a win come from tactics, technique, or reading
  an opponent rather than from a stat/tier advantage — the protagonist beats
  someone with a better number using something the number sheet does not
  measure.
- Introduce rivalry through a loss that also creates a social hook (notice,
  alliance, a formed group) in the same scene, so defeat does not isolate.
  Let rivals keep moving off page; report or imply their advancement so the
  protagonist's climb reads against a moving target.
- Run a standing, rank-ordered ladder beneath the formal tournament so
  competitive stakes stay live between tournament beats. Let the tournament
  read as an escalation of a running mechanism, not a structure introduced
  from scratch at the climax. Seed the bracket from the same visible rank
  the reader has been tracking, so an upset reads as consistent with earlier
  information.
- Give at least one progress marker per arc through a channel other than the
  stat system — a reversal in how other characters address or treat the
  protagonist counts; a bare stat-sheet gain with no social echo does not.
- Montage/compression is for *quantitative* movement (numbers changing)
  only. Any scene that also claims to deliver new plot, world-rule, or
  emotional information must be rendered in full, not summarized.
- No more than one consecutive scene of pure setback — a loss or injury with
  no new tactical information and no relationship movement — before a
  partial win, a new insight, or a relationship beat arrives.

## Editor gates

1. **Stat-delta gate.** Every scene that advances a tracked stat/rank names
   the specific delta. No scene claims growth with only a vague assertion.
2. **Information gate.** Every ladder duel or tournament round changes the
   protagonist's information state — about an opponent, the system, or
   themselves — not merely their bracket position or win/loss tally.
3. **Cost-of-input gate.** Any stat/system growth tied to training shows
   what was expended to produce it. Growth with no shown cost fails.
4. **Skill-vs-stat gate.** At least one major win per arc is attributable to
   tactics/technique/read-the-opponent reasoning against a better-equipped
   opponent, not to a stat or rank advantage.
5. **Rival-motion gate.** Every named rival has their own on-page or
   credibly reported advancement within the arc the protagonist advances in.
   A rival who is static while the protagonist grows fails.
6. **Externally-legible-progress gate.** At least one progress marker per
   arc is visible through a channel other than the stat system (social
   treatment, a changed reputation, a reversal in address).
7. **Montage-scope gate.** Montage/compression covers only quantitative
   movement. A scene that also delivers new plot, world-rule, or emotional
   information is rendered in full.
8. **Setback-density gate.** No more than one consecutive scene of pure
   setback (no new information, no relationship movement) before a partial
   win, a tactical insight, or a relationship beat.
