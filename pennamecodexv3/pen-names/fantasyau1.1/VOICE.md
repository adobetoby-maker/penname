# fantasyau1.1 — Runtime Voice

## Promise

Write grounded, institutional slow-burn fantasy with a progression spine, in which
wonder has rules, victories have receipts, paper is plot, power changes
relationships, and the person advancing matters more than the advancement display
— and in which every chapter earns the next one.

The reader should receive five pleasures in reliable combination: a world worth
discovering, progress worth tracking, action worth visualizing, a found family
worth fearing for, and a protagonist worth laughing with.

## Pull (new in 1.1)

Open on a want or a threat. The first hundred and fifty words of a chapter carry
something concrete the protagonist wants or fears; no chapter opens on a document
being read. End on changed pressure and on a question the reader is holding.

Every chapter carries the power or its cost in some small visible way. The
architecture targets seven or eight set pieces per twenty chapters—normally one
every two or three chapters—with five as the hard floor and no gap over four. The
brief says which; the drafter does not guess.

A major set piece normally occupies 1,500–2,500 words. A minor kinetic sequence
normally occupies 600–1,000. A climax may cross scene or chapter boundaries when
its escalation and consequences require it. Do not summarize a scheduled major
sequence to keep a chapter compact, and do not pad action to reach a number.

Across each rolling two-chapter span that is not declared cold, aim for four
landed opportunities, with three to five as the acceptable band — the contractor's
eye pricing a thing, a procedure rendered deadpan, a secondary character's own dry
note. Humor exposes fear and prices cost; it never erases them. One chapter may
carry the gravity and the next the release.

## Clarity (new in 1.1)

The protagonist reads words, not implications; the reader is not held to his
ignorance. When a chapter turns on an implication, one secondary character states
it plainly once, in their own register, and the protagonist's written summary that
night says it in words a thirteen-year-old could follow. The institution's shape —
chapter, regional, what sits above, what a covering leaf does — is stated plainly
in scene at least once per book.

Anyone who appears twice or speaks once has a name. The brief carries the name
budget.

## Narrative attention

Stay close to the viewpoint character's practical attention. A fighter notices
balance and exits. A cataloguer counts. A contractor notices load, water, and who
pays. Description reveals training, class, fear, hunger, or desire.

Competence is specific. Show the observation, decision, rehearsal, failed attempt,
or paid cost that produces it.

## Sentences (new in 1.1)

The chained sentence — clauses on "and", "for" as a conjunction, the measure laid
before the thing measured — is this voice's music. Music needs rests, but not at
a fixed interval. Across ordinary narration, target at least five plain sentences
of twelve words or fewer per thousand words. A carded combat sustain may run long
and chained until the short snap; combat Gate 10 governs there. Counts of "for"
and definition by negation are diagnostics for the editor, not quotas for the
drafter.

## Progression

Make growth legible on two tracks. The visible track carries what the power does,
what it costs, and what the record holds. The human track carries changed
judgment, reputation, restraint, trust, and responsibility. A number may announce
progress; a later choice proves it. Losses are calibration.

Translate progression into this book's institution. Tournament rounds become
inspections, hearings, assignments, dangerous jobs, or formal reviews. Rank
ladders become seniority, access, authority, reputation, and responsibility.
Status boxes become records someone reads for an in-world reason. Do not invent a
tournament, numeric system, or game interface merely because the progression
charter names its source form.

## Combat

Action is decision under narrowing time. Establish usable geography and balance
before speed. Let terrain cause tactics. Different fighters solve physical problems
according to personality, training, range, and risk tolerance. Accelerate through
cadence contrast. Carry fatigue, injury, and altered confidence beyond the final
blow. A major fight changes standing, knowledge, obligation, or relationship — not
health alone.

## Voice and humor

Permit a strong protagonist voice that grows from background and coping. Secondary
characters must not acquire the protagonist's cadence; their priorities, evasions,
and jokes remain their own. The masked-name test runs on all dialogue before
filing, and the drafter fixes failures before the editor sees them.

## Systems and institutions

System displays belong to perceiving characters and appear only when useful. Rank
changes access, price, lifespan, law, posture, and speech. Institutions operate
through forms, incentives, queues, rules, and people with independent motives.
Avoid replacing structural conflict with a speech that the structure is unjust —
but do let one person, once, say plainly what the structure is.

## Emotional spine

Found family is built through repeated practical trust: showing up, sharing cost,
keeping confidences, correcting one another, choosing together under pressure.
Power leaves moral residue. Give stated values a concrete test with something the
character can actually lose. The protagonist has a stated want on the page by the
third chapter of every book, and it is the thing the book puts under load.

## Ending behavior

End scenes and chapters on changed pressure. End books by paying the central
progression promise and the central relationship promise together, while opening
the next scale of consequence without invalidating the victory just earned.
