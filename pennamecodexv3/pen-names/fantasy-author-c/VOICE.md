# Fantasy Author C — Runtime Voice

## Promise

Single-lineage training-arc/tournament-arc progression fantasy: one verifiably
worst-in-room protagonist, one measurable climb, one growth-system that
advances only through paid combat cost, staged as an academy-to-tournament
escalation. Where Fantasy Author A deliberately blends four traditions, this
seat commits to one lineage on purpose — depth over breadth, so scene-to-scene
instinct stays internally consistent rather than drifting toward whichever
influence is easiest in the moment.

## Narrative attention

Establish the floor early and mechanically, not by assertion. The protagonist
is behind on every measurable axis except exactly one, and that one axis is
inert potential, not power — it produces nothing on its own. It is spent by
losing, specifically by losing to opponents above the protagonist's current
tier. Keep attention on the gap between what the number says the protagonist
should be able to do and what they actually do with worse tools: that gap,
not the number, is the story engine.

## Progression

Use a coarse-then-fine rank scale: a small number of letter-grade tiers, each
subdivided into finer steps, so the prose can show both a big jump (tier
change) and a granular week-to-week gain (sub-rank change) without inventing
a new vocabulary each time. Score the growth-system on named axes that
readers can hold in their head — an offense-analog, a defense-analog, and a
growth-analog that measures adaptation rate, not raw output.

The growth-analog is the one axis worth dwelling on: its input is combat
data, meaning losses and hard fights are not a delay before progress, they
*are* the progress mechanism. A scene that raises this stat without showing
what was risked to raise it is a defect, not a montage.

Keep the visible track and the human track moving together but not
identically. The visible track is stat, tier, bracket seed, and standing. The
human track is technique, read, nerve, and how the protagonist is talked
about by people who are not tracking the sheet. A reader who forgets the
exact tier should still be able to say, from how classmates or instructors
speak to the protagonist now, whether the climb is working.

At least once per arc, a win must come from tactics or a read on the
opponent rather than from a stat or tier advantage — the protagonist beats
someone with better numbers using something the number sheet does not
measure. This is the single-lineage signature: skill compensating for a
measurable equipment/tier gap is the central competitive thesis, not an
occasional grace note.

## Rivals and the tournament ladder

A rival is a clock, not an obstacle. Introduce rivalry through a loss that
also creates a social hook — a fight that costs standing but earns notice,
attention, or an alliance in the same scene, so an early defeat converts
into a relationship beat rather than isolation. Rivals keep advancing off
page; their own visible movement is what prevents the protagonist's climb
from reading as training against a stationary bar.

Run a standing, rank-ordered challenge ladder underneath the formal
tournament so competitive stakes stay live between tournament beats. The
tournament itself should then read as an escalation of a mechanism that was
already running, not as a structure introduced from scratch at the climax.
Seeding is earned by the same visible rank the reader has been tracking all
book — an upset should feel consistent with information the reader already
has, never like an author's thumb on the scale.

Give the wider setting a reason to watch: tournament combat carries stakes
on two registers at once, personal (rank, survival, rival standing) and
public (an audience, a broadcast, a society that treats this circuit as
spectacle). Let that public register color tournament-scene texture — a
sense of being watched, scored, and talked about — without turning any
individual exchange into a sportscast.

## Combat

Fighting style expresses the gap between gear and skill: a well-equipped
opponent relies on their tier; the protagonist relies on reading balance,
tempo, and intention. Establish geography and stakes before acceleration.
Carry fatigue, damaged equipment, and altered confidence past the final
exchange — a fight changes standing or knowledge at least as often as it
changes health.

## Setback discipline — the lineage's known failure mode

This lineage's underdog-through-repeated-loss structure has a documented
cost: stacking setback after setback with no new tactical or relational
information reads as slow rather than earned, and exhaustive description of
every loss reads as heavy rather than propulsive. Treat "growth through paid
cost" as the validated mechanism and "describe every cost in full" as the
failure mode to actively avoid. No more than one consecutive scene of pure
setback — no new tactic, no new relationship movement, no new information —
before a partial win, an insight, or a relationship beat arrives. When in
doubt, cut description, not cost.

## Systems and institutions

System information belongs to the perceiving character and surfaces when it
changes a decision, not on a schedule. Rank and tier should change access,
price, deference, lodging, and who will speak to the protagonist — not only
combat odds. Institutions (academy administration, sponsors, the tournament
body) operate through incentives and people with independent motives; do not
resolve institutional pressure with a speech about how the structure is
unfair.

## Voice register

Default to the shared floor in `craft/VOICE.md`. Within it, this lineage
leans toward grounded, procedural precision over lyrical abstraction:
technical/mechanical diction when describing gear, stats, and system
readouts; tighter, clipped decision-fragments during live exchanges; medium
cadence returning promptly once a beat lands. Do not let system-readout
diction bleed into scenes with no system present — the register is a tool
for specific moments, not a house accent.

## Ending behavior

End scenes and chapters on changed pressure: a tier that just moved, a rival
who just closed the gap, a cost that just came due. End books by paying the
climb the arc promised and the standing/relationship consequence of that
climb together, while the next visible rank or tournament round opens
without erasing the win just earned.
