# Science-Fiction Author B — Clean-Room Craft Research

Compiled: 2026-08-30. Purpose: design provenance for a new pen-name profile.
No published fiction was ingested or quoted. The sources are author interviews,
official craft essays/FAQs, and a craft podcast discussing a published framework.
Runtime prompts contain only the synthesized mechanisms, never influence names.

## 1. Character exists in relationship

Orson Scott Card describes two linked characterization rules: each character has
their own purposes, and personality changes according to relationship. Operationally,
every major character needs an independent agenda and at least two relationship
contexts that expose different strategies or vulnerabilities.

Primary interview:
https://www.writerswrite.com/journal/orson-scott-card-9992

## 2. Moral fiction asks what is worth suffering for

Card frames fiction's moral value around why people act, what deserves to be shown,
and what is worth suffering for. He separately describes the common human dilemma as
good versus good under uncertain outcomes and real price. Operationally, a major
choice should preserve at least two live goods, assign cost, and leave residue after
the outcome.

Primary interview archive:
https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-16-3775t3g838

## 3. Story promises close according to their type

The MICE framework distinguishes milieu, inquiry/idea, character, and event threads.
Its useful operational claim is not taxonomy for its own sake: the condition that
opens a thread predicts the kind of closure the reader is owed. A place-entering
thread closes through leaving or belonging; a question through answer or accepted
unknowability; a character dissatisfaction through change or acceptance; a disruptive
event through a new stable condition.

Craft discussion of Card's published framework:
https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/

## 4. Speculation generates the problem

Andy Weir describes beginning with scientific speculation—mission design, an off-world
city, or spacecraft fuel—and discovering story by following what could go wrong and
what consequences emerge. Operationally, Author B begins with one or two changes,
derives problems from them, and resists adding unrelated wonders merely for scale.

Primary interview:
https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/articles/andy-weir-interview/

## 5. Research is deep, but explanation is selective

Weir reports deep research across physics, relativity, biology, chemistry, and
materials science while also discussing the need to restrain technical information.
He keeps as much science real as possible around the declared fictional premise.
Operationally, the bible labels fact, extrapolation, and invention; prose includes
only what changes a prediction, choice, repair, or relationship.

Primary interview:
https://www.space.com/project-hail-mary-andy-weir-book-interview

## 6. Humor can carry exposition but cannot replace correctness

Weir calls humor a tool for making necessary exposition easier to receive. The
transferable mechanism is pressure-shaped explanation: wit, teaching, frustration,
or interpersonal asymmetry makes information human. The harness adds a safeguard:
humor never excuses a missing causal step or makes every expert share one voice.

Primary interview:
https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/authors/interviews/article/85632-weir-d-science-pw-talks-with-andy-weir.html

## 7. Major beats fixed; route allowed to improve

Weir reports knowing major beats, twists, and the ending while leaving enough room
for better discoveries during drafting. Sanderson likewise describes extensive but
living outlines and notes that foreshadowing and multi-book work benefit from planning.
Operationally, lock the ending problem, decisive choice, and reveal order; permit the
scene route to change when it improves causality without breaking those commitments.

Primary interviews/official FAQ:
https://www.grimdarkmagazine.com/an-interview-with-andy-weir/
https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/are-you-an-outliner-or-a-discovery-writer/

## 8. Solutions require reader-legible rules

Sanderson's first law ties conflict resolution to reader understanding. The second
prioritizes limitations over powers; the third asks writers to expand existing
elements before adding new ones. For science fiction, the same logic governs devices,
biology, alien capabilities, institutions, and speculative physics.

Official source:
https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/what-are-sandersons-laws-of-magic/

## 9. Worldbuilding must touch character and plot

Sanderson advises focusing development on aspects that affect the story and tying
setting to characters and conflict. Operationally, a technical or cultural detail
earns page space by changing access, danger, belief, labor, relationship, or choice.

Official source:
https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/do-you-have-advice-about-worldbuilding/

## 10. Revision is not repeated reinvention

Both discarded material and rewriting are legitimate when a draft fails its intended
book. Weir describes shelving a 70,000-word project while salvaging compatible parts;
Sanderson distinguishes front-loaded outlining from back-loaded rewriting and warns
that both still require polishing. The harness conclusion is bounded specialization:
repair a verified structural failure, preserve working material, and stop when exit
criteria pass rather than reopening the whole manuscript.

Primary interview/official FAQ:
https://www.space.com/project-hail-mary-andy-weir-book-interview
https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/can-you-go-into-depth-about-outlining/

## Runtime synthesis

Author B therefore combines:

- relationship-defined character;
- morally costly choice under uncertainty;
- typed story promises and honest closure;
- scientific problem generation and hypothesis revision;
- accessible but causally rigorous explanation;
- earned optimism and cooperation;
- reader-legible speculative rules and limitations;
- an ending-aware but flexible drafting process.

These mechanisms are not instructions to reproduce any source author's prose.
