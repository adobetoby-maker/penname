numbered principles are carried forward below with their existing [SOURCED]

docking interface, gates — is [INFERRED] unless a fresh citation is given (six

inward-facing (character, morality, promise-typing); Weir's (4–6) are

## 1. Pillar I — Character and Moral Choice (Card)

### 1.1 Relational Character
Personality is not fixed; it is expressed differently depending on who a
character is with, and every major character pursues a personal agenda that
exists independent of the protagonist's arc. [SOURCED — brief §1; primary
interview, writerswrite.com/journal/orson-scott-card-9992]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Independent Agenda Test** — every major character's want must be
  statable in one sentence that does not reference the protagonist's goal.
  If the character's only describable want is "help/hinder the protagonist,"
  the agenda is not yet independent. [SOURCED]
- **(b) Two-Context Rule** — every major character needs at least two
  distinct relationship contexts on the page (e.g., with a superior, with an
  equal, with someone they are protecting or competing against) that expose
  different strategies or vulnerabilities. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Personality-Under-Pressure Shift** — track, in the state ledger,
  whether the character's presented self measurably changes between those
  two contexts. Identical behavior in both is a sign the second context is
  decorative rather than load-bearing. [INFERRED]

### 1.2 Good vs. Good, Priced
Fiction's moral value lives in why people act and what is worth suffering
for; the central human dilemma worth dramatizing is good versus good under
uncertain outcomes and real price. [SOURCED — brief §2; primary interview
archive, americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-16-3775t3g838]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Two Live Goods Test** — before drafting a major moral-choice scene,
  name the two goods in conflict. If one option is secretly bad dressed as
  good, the scene is disguised melodrama, not good-versus-good. [SOURCED]
- **(b) Priced Choice** — the choice must cost something specific and named
  (a relationship, a resource, standing, a piece of self-image) — not merely
  read as emotionally heavy. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Residue Requirement** — the named cost must be referenced again later
  in the book or series. A moral choice whose price is never mentioned again
  was not actually priced — it was decorated. [INFERRED]
- **(d) Intention Is Not Acquittal** — a guardrail derived from documented
  criticism of this exact mechanism in the influence's own signature work
  (see Failure-Mode 6.4 below): a character's good intention explains a
  choice but does not exempt it from consequence on the page. The narrative
  must show a consequence that exists independent of how pure the stated
  motive was. [INFERRED — guardrail]

### 1.3 Typed Promises, Honest Closure
Story threads open as one of four types — milieu, inquiry/idea, character,
event — and the opening condition predicts the closure type the reader is
owed. [SOURCED — brief §3; craft discussion of Card's published framework,
writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Opening Tag** — every thread is tagged at the moment it opens: M
  (place entered), I (question posed), C (character dissatisfaction), or E
  (disruption). [SOURCED]
- **(b) Matched Closure** — M closes on leaving-or-belonging; I closes on
  answer-or-accepted-unknowability; C closes on change-or-accepted-status-quo;
  E closes on a new stable condition. Closing with the wrong type's payoff is
  a mismatch, not an ending. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Nesting Discipline** — threads may nest (an Event thread opened
  inside a Milieu thread), but each nested thread is tracked and closed on
  its own terms. An outer closure does not silently discharge an inner one.
  [INFERRED]

## 2. Pillar II — Speculative Method (Weir)

### 2.1 Speculation Generates the Problem
Story begins with scientific speculation — mission design, an off-world
city, spacecraft fuel — and the story is discovered by following what could
go wrong and what consequences follow. [SOURCED — brief §4; primary
interview, penguinrandomhouse.com/articles/andy-weir-interview]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) One-or-Two-Change Rule** — begin with exactly one or two deliberate
  departures from known reality. Every plot problem must be derivable from
  those changes, not imported from an unrelated new wonder. [SOURCED]
- **(b) No Wonder-for-Scale** — resist adding an additional marvel purely to
  raise spectacle. If a speculative element doesn't generate a problem or a
  consequence, cut it. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Consequence Chaining** — before drafting, walk each speculative
  change forward through at least two downstream consequences (what breaks,
  who is endangered, what must be improvised). [INFERRED]

### 2.2 Fact / Extrapolation / Invention Ledger
Deep research across physics, relativity, biology, chemistry, and materials
science sits alongside a deliberate restraint on how much of it appears on
the page; the goal is to keep as much science real as possible around a
declared fictional premise. [SOURCED — brief §5; primary interview,
space.com/project-hail-mary-andy-weir-book-interview]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Three-Tier Labeling** — every technical claim in the universe bible
  is labeled ESTABLISHED FACT, PLAUSIBLE EXTRAPOLATION, or INVENTED PREMISE.
  [SOURCED]
- **(b) Selective Surfacing Test** — a technical explanation earns page
  space only if it changes a prediction, a choice, a repair, or a
  relationship. Explanation that does none of these is cut regardless of how
  accurate or interesting it is. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Research-to-Prose Ratio** — the depth of research behind a fact may
  (and should) exceed what appears on the page; the page shows only the
  load-bearing fraction. [INFERRED]

### 2.3 Humor as Pressure-Shaped Explanation
Humor is a tool for making necessary exposition easier to receive; the
transferable mechanism is that wit, teaching, frustration, or interpersonal
asymmetry makes information feel human rather than lectured. [SOURCED —
brief §6; primary interview, publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/authors/
interviews/article/85632-weir-d-science-pw-talks-with-andy-weir.html]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Delivery-Vehicle Test** — humor, teaching tone, frustration, or
  interpersonal asymmetry are the permitted vehicles for exposition;
  deadpan lecture with no character pressure behind it is not. [SOURCED]
- **(b) No Missing-Step License** — humor may make an explanation easier to
  receive; it may never stand in for a missing causal step in the science.
  [INFERRED — harness safeguard, stated explicitly in the brief itself]
- **(c) Voice Differentiation Guardrail** — a guardrail derived from
  documented criticism (see Failure-Mode 6.3 below) that the same
  comic-explainer voice recurred across different protagonists in different
  books: every character delivering comic exposition must retain their own
  diction and rhythm. Two named characters whose comic-exposition dialogue is
  interchangeable under a masked-name test fail this sub-principle.
  [INFERRED — guardrail]

## 3. Pillar III — World-Law, Structure, and Process (Sanderson + Weir/Sanderson process)

### 3.1 Reader-Legible Rules and Limitations
Conflict resolution is tied to reader understanding (First Law); limitations
matter more than powers (Second Law); existing elements are expanded before
new ones are added (Third Law) — and for science fiction the same logic
governs devices, biology, alien capabilities, institutions, and speculative
physics, not only magic systems. [SOURCED — brief §8; official source,
faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/what-are-sandersons-laws-of-magic]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) First-Law Test, Ported** — a problem may be resolved by a device,
  biology, alien capability, institution, or speculative-physics rule only in
  proportion to how well the reader already understands that rule.
  Capabilities that CREATE problems are exempt; capabilities that RESOLVE
  them are not (direct port of the fantasy-charter fairness law). [SOURCED]
- **(b) Limitations-Over-Powers, Ported** — every named capability or
  technology states what it cannot do, or what it costs, by its second
  on-page use. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Extend-Before-Invent** — before introducing a new device,
  institution, or alien biology, check whether an existing one can be
  extended, recombined, or given a new consequence to serve the same
  narrative need. [SOURCED]

### 3.2 Worldbuilding Touches Character and Plot
Development effort concentrates on aspects that affect the story, tying
setting to characters and conflict rather than developing the world for its
own sake. [SOURCED — brief §9; official source, faq.brandonsanderson.com/
knowledge-base/do-you-have-advice-about-worldbuilding]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Six-Function Test** — a technical or cultural detail earns page
  space only if it changes one of: access, danger, belief, labor,
  relationship, or choice for a named character. Otherwise it belongs in the
  bible, not the manuscript. [SOURCED]
- **(b) Character-Anchored Exposition** — worldbuilding is delivered through
  a character's stake in it (what it costs them, what it lets them do), not
  as an independent guided tour of the setting. [INFERRED]
- **(c) Bible/Page Split** — comprehensive worldbuilding lives in the
  universe bible; the manuscript surfaces only the load-bearing fraction —
  the same selectivity principle as §2.2's fact/extrapolation ledger, applied
  at the institutional/cultural scale instead of the technical scale.
  [INFERRED]

### 3.3 Fixed Beats, Flexible Route
Major beats, twists, and the ending are known in advance, while enough room
is left for better discoveries during drafting; extensive but living
outlines support foreshadowing and multi-book planning. [SOURCED — brief §7;
primary interview + official FAQ, grimdarkmagazine.com/an-interview-with-
andy-weir and faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/are-you-an-outliner-
or-a-discovery-writer]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Locked Commitments** — the ending problem, the decisive choice, and
  the reveal order are fixed before drafting begins. [SOURCED]
- **(b) Living Route** — the scene-by-scene path to those commitments may
  change during drafting when a better causal route is discovered, without
  renegotiating the locked commitments themselves. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Foreshadow Debt Carries Forward** — because endings are fixed early,
  foreshadowing can and must be planted across the full manuscript span, not
  improvised late. An un-seeded fixed beat is a planning failure, not a
  discovery-writing virtue. [INFERRED]

### 3.4 Revision as Bounded Specialization
Both discarding material and rewriting are legitimate when a draft fails its
intended book; front-loaded outlining and back-loaded rewriting are
distinguished, and both still require polishing afterward. [SOURCED — brief
§10; primary interview + official FAQ, space.com/project-hail-mary-andy-
weir-book-interview and faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/can-you-go-
into-depth-about-outlining]

**Sub-principles:**
- **(a) Diagnose-Before-Rewrite** — before any large revision, name the
  specific structural failure the draft exhibits. Revision addresses that
  named failure, not a general "make it better" impulse. [SOURCED]
- **(b) Preserve-What-Works** — compatible material (scenes, worldbuilding,
  character work) is salvaged from a failed draft rather than discarded
  wholesale. [SOURCED]
- **(c) Exit Criteria Stop the Loop** — revision passes stop when declared
  exit criteria pass. Reopening a manuscript section whose declared checks
  already passed, absent a newly discovered failure, is a process error.
  [INFERRED]

## 4. Cross-Pillar Synthesis — Where the Principles Interlock

**4.1 Moral cost as a speculative consequence (Card 1.2 × Weir 2.1c).** The
priced moral choice (1.2b) can — and ideally does — surface as one of the
downstream consequences chained out of the core speculative change (2.1c).
This makes the science and the ethics the same causal chain rather than two
parallel plots: the reactor that must be jettisoned IS the friendship that
must be sacrificed, not a coincidence running alongside it.

**4.2 Fairness law extended to ethics-resolving tech (Sanderson 3.1a × Card
1.2).** When a moment of moral reckoning is resolved BY a piece of
technology or an alien capability (a translator that reveals true intent, a
device that removes an temptation), the First-Law test (3.1a) applies to
that resolution exactly as it would to a combat or plot resolution: the
capability's rules must already be legible to the reader before it is used
to let a character off a moral hook. An unseeded moral-resolution device is
a Deus Ex Wrench wearing an ethics costume.

**4.3 MICE closure requires locked commitments (Card 1.3 × Sanderson/Weir
3.3a).** A fixed ending (3.3a) is, among other things, a commitment to how
each open MICE thread will close. Discovery writing (3.3b) may vary the
route to that closure but may not vary the closure TYPE without an explicit,
logged exception — an Inquiry thread does not quietly become a Character
thread because the route wandered.

**4.4 One selectivity principle, two scales (Weir 2.2b × Sanderson 3.2a).**
The Selective Surfacing Test (does this explanation change a prediction,
choice, repair, or relationship?) and the Six-Function Test (does this
worldbuilding change access, danger, belief, labor, relationship, or choice?)
are the same filter running at two different scales — technical fact
selection and cultural/institutional fact selection. A drafting agent should
treat them as one muscle: "does this earn its page space by changing
something a character does" — asked once of a fact and once of a custom.

**4.5 Humor's function shifts with relational context (Weir 2.3 × Card
1.1b).** The Two-Context Rule (1.1b) predicts that a character's
comic-exposition register will differ depending on who they are explaining
to — an engineer explaining a problem to a superior officer performs
differently than the same engineer explaining it to an equal they are
falling for. Voice Differentiation (2.3c) and Personality-Under-Pressure
(1.1c) are checked together: the same character, two contexts, two
measurably different explanatory registers.

**4.6 Bounded revision meets the allowance ledger (Sanderson/Weir 3.4a ×
Weir 2.2a).** When revision uncovers a science error, the fix is scoped: it
means re-labeling or correcting the affected ledger entries in the
fact/extrapolation/invention table (2.2a) and anything drafted downstream of
them — not reopening the whole manuscript. This is what "bounded" means in
practice for a science-first genre: the blast radius of a research
correction is measured against the ledger, not guessed at.

**4.7 Intention-is-not-acquittal is a cross-pillar test, not just a Card
rule (Card 1.2d × Sanderson 3.1b).** A capability's "cost" (3.1b) and a
choice's "residue" (1.2c) are the same mechanism at two scales: technology
that resolves a problem still has to have paid for itself in constraint, and
a decision that resolves a moral dilemma still has to have paid for itself
in consequence. Neither a technology nor a good intention is a free pass.

## 5. Failure-Mode Guardrails

Six documented, citable failure modes — three from Weir's published-work
reception, two from critical scholarship on Card, one from Sanderson's SF
crossover work. Each is genuinely citable; none was fabricated. Sourced via
WebSearch on 2026-09-04 specifically for this task.

**5.1 The competent-man archetype flattens interiority (Weir).** Documented:
Weir has discussed in interviews that character development is a weak point
for him — his process starts from the science problem and builds characters
around it afterward. Independent review commentary describes his characters
as embodying a "competent man" archetype "defined less by interiority than
by their capacity to solve any problem given sufficient time," with the
"problem-fact-solution" structure crowding out other narrative dimensions
and emotional stakes rarely extending beyond generalized longing for Earth.
[Sources: thevaultpublication.com/2021/11/09/andy-weirs-project-hail-marys-
its-science-nerd-junk-food-but-its-still-nutritious; mythcreants.com/blog/
andy-weirs-novels-are-full-of-social-commentary; secondary discussion citing
Weir's own interviews at threads.com/@matthewfabb/post/DWjRk1hlaXx]
Guardrail: Gate 10 (Interiority Independent of Problem) — every major
sequence needs at least one beat establishing character stake that is not
itself the technical problem.

**5.2 Humanizing over-correction produces an inconsistent emotional
register (Weir).** Documented: reviewers of Project Hail Mary described
protagonist Ryland Grace as having "basically only two emotions: childlike
excitement and whiny sadness," calling him "a whiny, immature man-child";
comparative reviews of The Martian noted protagonist Mark Watney "spoke and
acted like a texting fourteen-year-old boy" such that "it was hard to
believe he was a NASA astronaut." [Sources:
jeroenthoughts.wordpress.com/2021/05/26/review-andy-weir-project-hail-
mary-2021; deathisbad.substack.com/p/sff-review-the-martian] Guardrail: Gate
1 (Relational Character Test, 1.1a/b) plus a consistency check — a
character's emotional register in a scene must be plausible given their
established competence and backstory, not simply "young and snarky" as a
default humanizing move.

**5.3 A single narrator voice recurs across different protagonists and
books (Weir).** Documented: critics observed that the protagonist's
"snarky, hipshot, goofy voice" in Artemis was "much the same as Mark
Watney's" in The Martian, and that Artemis's female protagonist "happens to
think, talk and act like a geeky 45-year-old" — an outlook "overly
manufactured" that undermined the humor. Weir was noted to be "following a
template" of an ultra-nerdy narrator across separate books. [Sources:
mcphedranbadside.com/andy-weirs-artemis-all-that-is-wrong-with-sci-fi-
writing; npr.org/2017/11/15/562555478/andy-weir-aims-to-duplicate-his-
martian-success-with-artemis] Guardrail: Gate 9 (Voice Distinctness Under
Humor, 2.3c) — a masked-name test on comic-exposition dialogue across
different named characters and, at series level, across different
protagonists.

**5.4 Good intentions function as narrative acquittal for atrocity (Card).**
Documented: in the widely cited essay "Creating the Innocent Killer: Ender's
Game, Intention, and Morality" (Foundation: The International Review of
Science Fiction, Vol. 33, No. 90, Spring 2004), SF scholar John Kessel
argues Card's fiction constructs an "innocent killer" — a character guilty
of tremendous violence (including genocide) who is treated as morally
unculpable because his intentions were pure, when "in the real world there
is no such thing as an innocent killer." [Sources:
goodreads.com/book/show/8565607-creating-the-innocent-killer;
masson.us/blog/enders-game-creating-the-innocent-killer;
johnjosephkessel.wixsite.com/kessel-website/creating-the-innocent-killer]
Guardrail: Gate 4 (Intention-Consequence Split, 1.2d) — an action justified
by good intention must still show an on-page consequence that exists
independent of the stated purity of that intention.

**5.5 Didactic sympathy-demand undercuts the good-versus-good premise
(Card).** Documented: scholarship on Card's moral framework notes his
fiction is built on a "morality of intention" — judging characters by who
they are and what they meant rather than what they did — and that this can
ask readers for automatic sympathy with a protagonist regardless of the
consequences of his actions, tipping "good versus good" into one-sided
rhetoric rather than a dramatized dilemma. [Sources:
digitalcommons.cedarville.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1013&context=
english_seminar_capstone; shunn.substack.com/p/i-ender-having-been-born-of-
goodly] Guardrail: Gate 2 (Two Live Goods Test, 1.2a) plus a dramatization
requirement — the opposing good's strongest proponent must get an on-page
dramatized scene showing their reasoning in action, not a summarized or
other-character-reported position.

**5.6 Worldbuilding/exposition crowds out tension and stalls other threads
(Sanderson, SF crossover).** Documented: reviews of Sanderson's Skyward-
series novel Cytonic identified "lack of tension" as the book's "biggest
flaw" — "rarely did I feel like Spensa was in actual danger, so some moments
that were supposed to be big deals did not land" — while the book leaned on
"higher sci-fi concepts to explain the growth of these characters," and
several story arcs from earlier books were "set aside" rather than
advanced. [Sources: 17thshard.com/news/brandon-news/cytonic-review;
goodreads.com/book/show/57571215-cytonic — aggregated reader reviews]
Guardrail: Gate 13 (Six-Function Worldbuilding Test, 3.2a) plus a thread-
motion check derived from 1.3 — every open MICE thread must show forward
motion in any book/arc where new worldbuilding is introduced; worldbuilding
volume is not a substitute for thread progress.

## 6. The Docking Interface — SF Adaptation

### 6.0 The Speculative Allowance Table (SF's version of §7.1.1's
limitation/weakness/cost table)

Where the fantasy charter classifies every capability as hard/soft magic
with a limitation/weakness/cost entry, the SF charter classifies every
technical or scientific claim into one of three allowance tiers (direct
application of Pillar II §2.2a):

| Tier | Definition | Reader-legibility burden |
|---|---|---|
| **Established Fact** | Real, verifiable physics/biology/engineering as understood today | Correctness citation only; no in-world explanation debt |
| **Plausible Extrapolation** | A specified, named extension of established science along a stated vector (e.g., "fusion drives scaled past current efficiency ceilings by X") | Must name the extrapolation rule AND its boundary — the point past which it stops being plausible and becomes invention |
| **Invented Premise** | Purely fictional (FTL, exotic physics, alien biology with no terrestrial analog) | Full fairness-law treatment from §3.1: reader-legible rule before it resolves a problem, plus a stated limitation/weakness/cost by its second on-page use — tracked exactly like a fantasy magic system |

Every named technology, phenomenon, alien capability, or institution in the
universe bible carries this tag. The tag determines how much explanation
debt the manuscript owes the reader before that element may be used to
resolve (not merely create) a problem.

### 6.1 The Five Artifacts, SF Version

1. **Universe bible.** The speculative allowance table (above) for every
   named element; for each Invented Premise, its limitation/weakness/cost
   entry (§3.1b); a discovery timeline/tech-tree (what is known, by whom,
   when); and the institutional-consequence table showing how each
   allowance-tier element intersects economics, law, labor, or belief
   (§3.2a) — the SF analogue of the fantasy bible's rank/non-combat
   consequence table.
2. **Arc document.** Locked commitments (§3.3a): the ending problem, the
   decisive choice, and the reveal order. Per-act promise inventory. The
   moral-cost stake and its checkpoints (§1.2b–c). The MICE thread registry
   (opens/closes by type, §1.3). The plant→payoff map covering BOTH
   speculative reveals and moral consequences — a single map, not two,
   per Cross-Pillar 4.3.
3. **Chapter cards.** Opening promise in one sentence; owed delivery type
   (payoff | progress); MICE thread tags opened/closed; any Invented-Premise
   usage citing its bible constraint entry; and, for any new technical
   explanation included, a one-line answer to the Selective Surfacing Test
   (§2.2b) — what prediction, choice, repair, or relationship does this
   explanation change?
4. **Name registry.** Aurally distinct names (Process Law §8.1, unchanged —
   genre-agnostic); additionally, each named technology/institution/alien
   entity is tagged with its allowance tier so the drafting agent (and,
   internally, the editor) always knows which things are load-bearing real
   science versus invented premise, independent of whether that distinction
   is ever surfaced to the reader.
5. **State ledger.** Open MICE threads by type; the moral-cost stake's
   current condition; the plant/payoff tracker covering both science reveals
   and ethical consequences (one ledger, per 4.3); a consequence log for
   every Invented-Premise usage (has its stated cost/limitation been paid on
   the page yet?); and an interiority-checkpoint log tracking whether each
   act contains a beat establishing character stake independent of the
   technical problem (direct guardrail against Failure-Mode 5.1).

### 6.2 Dock Checks (parallel to §7.2)
A chapter may be drafted only when: its card exists; every Invented-Premise
element it uses appears in the bible with its allowance tier and constraint
entry; every major character appearing has an independent agenda on record
(§1.1a); the state ledger is current through the previous chapter.

### 6.3 Precedence
Identical to THE_AUTHOR.md §7.4: canon (universe bible) > this charter > arc
document > chapter card > instinct.

## 7. Process Law — Notes for the Drafting Agent

THE_AUTHOR.md's Process Law (§8: audio-first rules, anti-tic law,
scene-vs-summary law) is explicitly genre-agnostic pipeline experience, not
brief-derived — it should carry forward into THE_SCIFI_AUTHOR.md largely
unchanged. Two narrow, clearly-optional SF-specific notes for the drafting
agent to weigh (neither is brief-sourced; flag both as proposals, not
mandates, if adopted):

- **Telemetry/readout diegesis (optional extension of §8.1.3).** THE_AUTHOR's
  "system text must read aloud" rule (written for LitRPG status boxes) has an
  obvious SF analogue: computer readouts, mission telemetry, AI-companion
  dialogue, and sensor logs should be drafted to be spoken aloud by a
  narrator — no tables-as-prose, no symbol soup. This is a genuinely useful
  port but was not in the SF brief; the drafting agent should decide whether
  to include it as an explicit gate or leave it implicit under the existing
  audio-first rules.
- **Technical-vocabulary tic check (optional extension of §8.2).** A
  jargon term or explanatory catchphrase used identically more than 3
  times per book is the SF-domain instance of the existing anti-tic law;
  no new rule is needed, just an explicit note in the ledger's
  phrase-frequency table that technical vocabulary is in scope, not only
  somatic/beat phrases.

No other Process Law changes are proposed. Audio-first, anti-tic, and

## 8. Proposed Editor's Unified Gate — 16 Gates

Deduplicated, binary, traceable list. Roughly proportional to fantasy's 26
gates across 4 pillars (16 gates / 3 pillars ≈ 5.3/pillar vs. fantasy's
6.5/pillar — slightly leaner per pillar since this charter has fewer,
denser principles per pillar).

**Character and moral gates (Pillar I — Card)**

1. **Relational Character Test.** Every major character's want is statable
   independent of the protagonist's goal (1.1a), AND shows measurably
   different behavior across ≥2 relationship contexts (1.1b). FAIL if either
   clause fails. [Card 1.1]
2. **Two Live Goods.** Every major moral-choice scene names two goods in
   conflict before drafting. FAIL if one option is secretly villainous
   dressed as good. [Card 1.2a]
3. **Priced-and-Referenced Choice.** Every major moral choice costs
   something specific and named (1.2b) AND that cost is referenced again
   later in the book/series (1.2c). FAIL if the cost is vague or never
   mentioned again. [Card 1.2b–c]
4. **Intention-Consequence Split.** An action justified by good intention
   still shows an on-page consequence independent of that intention. FAIL if
   consequence is waived because the motive was pure. [Card 1.2d; Failure-
   Mode 5.4]
5. **MICE Type-Match Closure.** Every thread's closing type matches its
   opening type's owed discipline (M/I/C/E). FAIL on mismatch. [Card 1.3a–b]

**Speculative-method gates (Pillar II — Weir)**

6. **Speculative Origin Traceability.** Every plot problem traces back to
   one of the declared one-or-two core speculative changes. FAIL if a
   problem is imported from an unrelated, untethered new wonder. [Weir
   2.1a–b]
7. **Allowance Ledger and Selective Surfacing.** Every technical claim
   appears in the bible tagged fact/extrapolation/invention (2.2a), AND
   every on-page technical explanation changes a prediction, choice, repair,
   or relationship (2.2b). FAIL if untagged, or if an explanation serves
   none of those four. [Weir 2.2a–b]
8. **Humor-Vehicle, No Missing Step.** Comic exposition is delivered via
   humor/teaching/frustration/asymmetry (2.3a), and no causal science step
   is skipped and covered by a joke (2.3b). FAIL if a joke stands in place
   of a causal link, or if a block of exposition carries no character-voiced
   pressure at all. [Weir 2.3a–b]
9. **Voice Distinctness Under Humor.** Masked-name test: two characters' (or
   two books' protagonists') comic-exposition dialogue is not
   interchangeable. FAIL if indistinguishable. [Weir 2.3c; Failure-Mode 5.3]
10. **Interiority Independent of Problem.** Each major sequence has ≥1 beat
    establishing character stake that is not itself the technical problem.
    FAIL if the character's only stated interior state IS the science
    problem. [Failure-Mode 5.1–5.2]

**World-law, structure, and process gates (Pillar III — Sanderson +
Weir/Sanderson process)**

11. **Solution Traceability and Constraint Tagging.** Every resolution by
    device/institution/alien capability cites the earlier passage
    establishing its rule (3.1a), AND every named Invented-Premise
    capability with ≥2 uses states a limitation/weakness/cost by its second
    use (3.1b). FAIL if either is missing. [Sanderson 3.1a–b]
12. **Extend-Before-Invent Ratio.** Per act, new named technologies or
    institutions introduced may not exceed existing ones extended by more
    than 2:1. FAIL if the ratio is exceeded. [Sanderson 3.1c]
13. **Six-Function Worldbuilding Test.** Worldbuilding passages over 150
    words change at least one of access, danger, belief, labor,
    relationship, or choice for a named character. FAIL if none apply.
    [Sanderson 3.2a; Failure-Mode 5.6]
14. **Locked-Commitment Integrity.** The ending problem, decisive choice,
    and reveal order match what was locked before drafting began. FAIL if a
    locked commitment silently changes without a logged, justified
    exception in the arc document. [Weir/Sanderson 3.3a]
15. **Foreshadow-Before-Reveal.** Every major reveal — speculative or moral
    — has ≥1 earlier plant with a lower chapter number. FAIL if absent.
    [Weir/Sanderson 3.3c]
16. **Bounded Revision.** Any large revision pass names the specific
    structural failure it addresses (3.4a) AND does not reopen a section
    whose declared exit criteria already passed without a newly logged
    failure (3.4c). FAIL if either clause fails. [Weir/Sanderson 3.4a, 3.4c]
