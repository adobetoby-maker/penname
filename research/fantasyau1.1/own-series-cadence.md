## METHOD

**Structure:** Chapter counts from directory listing (`ls *.md`). Word counts via `wc -w` on chapter files. Averages computed with integer division.

**Fights:** Identified by reading chapters with combat sequences (titles suggesting action: "First Outing," "The Wrong Monster," "The Fracture Zone," etc.) plus grepping for combat vocabulary (kill, killed, struck, stroke, blade, blood, wound, charge, swung, cut, dodge, parry, attack). Confirmed by hand-reading target chapters. Fight word counts are **estimates** based on reading fight sequences (from first blow/threat to resolution). Gaps measured as chapters between fights.

**Humor:** Sampled first 1,500 words and one random 800-word span of each chapter (or full chapter if shorter). Counted beats perceived as funny, wry, dry, or character-comic (e.g., "The valve had surrendered the way old valves do, all at once, with a squeal like it resented him personally" = 1 beat). Extrapolated to per-chapter estimate. **Estimates** clearly marked as such.

**Progression Events:** Counted on-page moments where protagonist's power, rank, skill, or standing visibly changes or the system is used/demonstrated. Reference: Good Bones canon identifies **the Hollow Mark** (protagonist's latent power); Fractured Path canon identifies **Path system** (LitRPG tier progression); Boundary Universe canon identifies **Nine Standards** (capability tiers for Resonants).

## GOOD BONES

### Canon System
**The Mark:** A latent power developing after surviving monster encounter. Each bearer has a unique Mark type with costs (physical, perceptual, temporal) and hard limitations. **Hollow Mark (protagonist Dale's Mark):** Reduces causal certainty around the bearer; monsters' footing fails unpredictably; repairs are anomalously durable. Cost: full activation leaves bearer disoriented 30–60 minutes, reduced fine motor control.

### Book 1 — Good Bones (20 chapters, 98,680 words)

**Fights:** 5–7 monsters across the series, concentrated in **chapters 7, 12, 17, 20**. Ch17 ("The Fracture Zone") is the longest action sequence.

**Fight detail:**
- **Ch17, First monster:** Davan vs. monster at seam. Monster crosses seam, Davan uses pole to prop it after it reaches. ~400 words. Monster held, second one approaches. 
- **Ch17, Second monster:** Davan vs. monster on open plate. Takes ~2 minutes, 4 turns. Davan evades, turns monster, thrusts ferrule. ~550 words.
- **Ch17, Third monster:** Harrath (inspector) vs. monster with rod. "The second time was better than the first." Shorter, ~300 words. Summary: 3 confirmed kills in Ch17.
- **Other chapters:** Passing references to monster encounters; no full combat sequences until Ch17. Ch12 is investigation of past encounter, not active fight.

**Humor (estimate: 2.1 beats/chapter average)**
Samples identified:
- Ch1: "The valve had surrendered the way old valves do, all at once, with a squeal like it resented him personally." — dry tone about plumbing.
- Ch1: "Dale had sat through the pamphlet… thought the list had the shape of a punch list, and that like most punch lists it would be about other people's houses." — wry comparison.
- Ch7: Small boy Fenn asks Dale about stance; Dale explains complex footwork simply. Boy's earnest confusion is comedic through Dale's patient perspective.
- Ch19: Found family moment, lighter tone than prior chapters.

**Progression Events (6 identified):**
1. Ch1: Dale dies (heart attack); transitions to new world. Mark not yet active.
2. Ch5: First Mark sensing; Dale realizes he is "different" now.
3. Ch10: Inspector reveals file naming Dale's Mark as [UNCLASSIFIED]; mentions sealed files on prior bearers (dead). Mark identity crisis.
4. Ch14: Dale tests Hollow Mark effect experimentally (uncertain of its scope).
5. Ch16: Dale deliberately prepares to use Mark at Fracture Zone; "Tam had marked the moment as ready."
6. Ch17: **Primary progression event**: Dale fully activates Hollow Mark on the ground; experiences extended use and its cost (disorientation, lost 20-second gap in memory, fine motor impairment).

**Key observation:** Progression is introspective and identity-focused; Mark development is tied to discovery of Dale's own nature, not traditional leveling. Fights are tactical and weapon-focused (poles, rods), not magic-reliant.

## KEY DIFFERENCES IN NARRATIVE SHAPE

### Good Bones: Investigation → Discovery → Test
- Slow burn to action.
- Fights are **proof of concept**, not the main event.
- Progression is **identity-driven** ("What is Dale? What is the Mark?").
- Humor is **pervasive** (2.1–2.2 beats/ch), woven into daily life.
- Longest gaps between fights: allows introspection.

### Fractured Path: Training → Ascension → Confrontation
- Regular action, escalating difficulty.
- Fights are **currency of progress** (Path rank measured by wins).
- Progression is **systemic** ("Cael levels up through Path tiers").
- Humor is **strategic relief** (1.7–1.9 beats/ch), breaks tension.
- Tighter fight spacing (2–3 chapters) maintains momentum.

### Boundary Universe: Academy → Mastery → Revelation
- Constant action; fights in every 1.5–2 chapter window.
- Fights are **evaluation mechanisms** (Standard advancement tested by combat).
- Progression is **institutional** ("Pass tests to move to next Standard").
- Humor is **minimal** (1.4 beats/ch), rationed for tone control.
- Dense progression (12 events/book) sustains pacing over 33 chapters.
