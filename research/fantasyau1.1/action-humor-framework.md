# Action and humor cadence — the benchmark framework for fantasyau1.1

Compiled 2026-09-07 by the lead from five Haiku studies in this folder (`hwfwm-`,
`salvatore-`, `sanderson-`, `iron-prince-`, `own-series-cadence.md`). Benchmark
fight and humor counts are mostly review-derived (LOW–MED confidence); structural
figures (chapters, words, laws, quoted craft rules) are HIGH. Own-series figures are
measured from the manuscripts by grep-then-read and sampled humor reads; the lead's
note on the Good Bones Book 2 row is below.

Reading the table: every benchmark, and two of the owner's three series, keeps a
set piece within **2–4 chapters** of the last one and lands **1–3 humor beats per
chapter**. Good Bones Book 2 is the outlier on action (a nine-chapter gap) while
being the funniest of the three on the sampled count. Boundary Universe is the
outlier on humor (1.4) while carrying the most action. Fractured Path already sits
inside the benchmark band on both.

## 2. What the best authors share (the framework)

Eight rules appear, in different words, in every study. Each is mapped to where
fantasyau1.1 enforces it.

| # | Rule | Who says it | 1.1 mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Promise, progress, payoff.** "The feeling of progress is what keeps the reader turning the page." (Sanderson) | Sanderson; Shirtaloon reviewers ("consequential downtime") | Charter §1.5; Pull gate 29 (want pressed every chapter); progress record per chapter |
| 2 | **A set piece every 2–4 chapters; never more than 4 without one.** | all four benchmarks; Fractured Path and Boundary already do it | Pull gate 30 — tightened from "every three-chapter window" to **every window of three, and never more than four chapters between set pieces across a book** |
| 3 | **Rules before payoff; limitations over powers; expand, don't add.** (Sanderson's three Laws) | Sanderson; Salvatore ("each blow counts") | Fairness Law §1.1, §1.2, §1.3 — unchanged; the fight auditor's FAIRNESS table |
| 4 | **Every fight changes something other than health, or it is cut.** "Fights that don't matter are dragged out." (Iron Prince complaint) | Iron Prince reviewers; VOICE §Combat | Fight audit paragraph "what changed"; a fight that changes nothing = MEDIUM |
| 5 | **Choreography before prose; feet, terrain, compression.** "I want you to see every blow." | Salvatore (rules 1–7) | Combat gates 9–16; Gate 10 tempo; Gate 16 terrain |
| 6 | **Humor comes from character and pairs with gravity; 1–3 beats a chapter, not 10.** | Salvatore (rules 8–9); Sanderson (Wayne/Wit as pressure release); HWFWM fatigue at 5–15 | Pull gate 31 (≥2 landed beats; ≤3 cold chapters) with a ceiling: **more than 5 beats in a chapter is itself a finding** |
| 7 | **The climax is a sequence, not a chapter.** 4–10 chapters (Sanderson); the "Sanderlanche" | Sanderson; Salvatore's multi-POV battles | Architecture: the climax window is 3–4 chapters in a 20-chapter book, converging threads, each chapter ending on a turn |
| 8 | **Downtime is consequential.** Slice-of-life and paper chapters earn their place by moving a promise or a relationship; otherwise they are the fatigue point. | Shirtaloon interview; HWFWM praise and complaints | Pull gate 29 + the two cold readers' skim reports; three chapters under 3 = STRUCTURAL |

Two benchmark habits do **not** transfer and are recorded so nobody imports them:
multi-POV rotation (Good Bones is single close third by Charter; Salvatore's
six-viewpoint battles are handled by sound, terrain, and reports at the place), and
system-text-as-pacing (the Guild's paper is diegetic and slow by design; the CAD/
HWFWM "system voice" cadence would break the book's register).

## 3. Numeric floors adopted into the 1.1 card template (from this evidence)

| Floor | 1.1 value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Set-piece spacing | one in every 3-chapter window; never >4 chapters between across a book | rule 2 |
| Set-piece length | major 1,200–2,500 words; minor 400–900; the climax sequence 3–4 chapters | Salvatore, Sanderson, own Boundary/FP figures |
| Fight consequence | every fight changes standing, knowledge, obligation, or relationship — named on the card before drafting | rule 4 |
| Humor | 2–5 landed beats per non-cold chapter (floor 2, ceiling 5); ≤3 cold chapters per book | rule 6 |
| Power visible | the power or its cost on the page every chapter; a full use at least every 6 chapters | rule 1, rule 3 |
| Chapter length | 4,000–5,500 words (all benchmarks and own series agree) | table |
| Climax | 3–4 chapter sequence, threads converging, each ending on a turn | rule 7 |
| Downtime | a paper/slice chapter must move a promise or a relationship, named on the card | rule 8 |

## 4. Where each of the owner's series sits against the floors

- **Good Bones:** action spacing fails (one nine-chapter gap in each book); humor at floor; power visibility fails between the two uses. Book 3's architecture is built on the 1.1 floors from card one.
- **Fractured Path:** inside every floor on action; humor at 1.6–1.9 sits just under the floor of 2 — the cheapest lift in the whole catalogue.
- **Boundary Universe:** action above the benchmark band (1.5–2 chapters between fights — Iron Prince territory, with the same complaint risk: fights that don't matter); humor at 1.4, the lowest measured. Its 1.1 cards need the fight-consequence rule and the humor floor more than anything else.

## 5. Confidence and what would raise it

Benchmark fight and humor counts came from reviews and wikis, not chapter-by-chapter tallies; treat them as bands. The own-series counts are measured but Haiku's fight definition was loose (it counted reported kills as fights). The next step that would harden the numbers is cheap: run the cold-reader seats over one benchmark book each (a public-domain-adjacent sample chapter set is enough) and over Good Bones Book 2, and compare their skim reports to these bands.
