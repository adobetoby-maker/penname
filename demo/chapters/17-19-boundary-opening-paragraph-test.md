Chapter 2 — Zero Protocol

By three in the afternoon,

Kade had been told not to leave Northline

by four different people

who all insisted he was not being detained.

This seemed to him like a distinction

invented by people who had never been detained.

He sat in Vice Principal Russell’s office

with Darius folded badly into the chair beside him

and Ms. Alvarez standing against the wall,

arms crossed.

Outside the narrow window,

two black government vehicles occupied spaces

normally reserved for district maintenance vans.

A third vehicle had arrived

without markings at all.

Darius tilted his head toward it.

“That one’s definitely secret.”

Kade looked through the blinds.

“Because it’s black?”

“Because it has no logo.”

“Maybe they’re embarrassed.”

“I’d be embarrassed if I drove that.”

“It probably costs more than your house.”

Darius considered the vehicle again.

“Then I’d be embarrassed with heated seats.”

Russell stopped pacing.

“Could both of you please understand

that this is serious?”

Darius looked at Kade.

Kade looked at Darius.

“No,” they said together.

Russell closed his eyes.

Ms. Alvarez’s mouth shifted

in what might have been an attempt not to smile.

Kade appreciated the effort.

The national testing network had come back online

nineteen minutes after it collapsed.

Everyone’s results had returned.

Everyone except his.

His candidate page now showed a gray rectangle

and four words.

RESULT WITHHELD — AUTHORIZED REVIEW.

That was somehow worse than zero.

Zero had been funny

for at least eight seconds.

Authorized review sounded expensive.

His phone buzzed on Russell’s desk.

Kade reached for it.

Russell planted one finger on the screen

before he could.

“No.”

“It’s my phone.”

“You have received forty-seven messages

in twenty minutes.”

The phone buzzed again.

“Forty-eight.”

“That is exactly my point.”

Darius leaned forward.

“Tell him about the video.”

Russell’s face changed.

Kade looked at Darius.

“What video?”

Darius became intensely interested

in the acoustic tile ceiling.

“Darius.”

“People filmed the network map.”

“People?”

“Some people.”

“How many?”

“Define many.”

Kade reached for the phone again.

Russell moved it farther away.

“Mr. Mercer,

until the district legal office tells me otherwise,

you are not posting anything,

answering questions,

or speaking to anyone claiming to represent a news organization.”
